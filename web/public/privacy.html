2. What stays on your iPhone

Locturne has no account and no server of its own for the app. Almost everything it handles is stored in the app’s private storage on your iPhone, and in an App Group: a storage area on your phone that only Locturne and its own extensions can read, so a block can start at bedtime while the app is closed. None of the following is sent to us.

What Why
Your app selections (as Apple’s sealed tokens) To block the apps, categories and websites you picked. See Screen Time.
Your routine: bedtime, morning start, which nights are on, wake-up method, step target, daily limits To know when to block and what wakes your apps.
Step counts from Apple’s motion sensors (Core Motion), counted from your morning start time For the “walk it off” wake-up. Locturne doesn’t use Apple Health.
Height changes from the barometer, during a short session of a few minutes that you start For the “go downstairs” wake-up. Only the change in height is used, not your location.
Which way the phone is facing (accelerometer) To turn the nap clock when you hold the phone sideways.
The camera, while you scan your code For the “scan your code” wake-up. The camera only reads the code. No photo or video is recorded or saved. The text of the code you register is stored so the morning scan can be matched to it.
Your mornings: when you proved you were up, and how (steps, stairs, scan, a pass or the emergency unlock) To wake your apps, count your monthly passes, and show Loc’s lines for firsts.
A short technical log of when iOS ran Locturne’s blocking (times, schedule names, whether a block was up) So Locturne can tell you honestly whether last night’s block started. It keeps about four nights and overwrites older entries.
Your answers in the setup questions To set up your routine and show your estimate. Some answers are also sent as analytics, see below.

Notifications (the bedtime heads-up, the morning nudge, the trial reminder and warnings) are scheduled on your iPhone. Locturne doesn’t send push notifications from a server.

Sharing is always your choice. When you tap a share button (for example on your morning card, your estimate or a beta diagnostics report), iOS’s share sheet opens and you choose where it goes. The diagnostics report contains technical details about schedules and blocks, not the apps you picked.

Ratings. Locturne may ask iOS to show Apple’s rating prompt. Apple handles it and decides whether it appears.

3. Screen Time

Locturne uses Apple’s Screen Time frameworks (Family Controls, Managed Settings and Device Activity) with your permission. When you pick apps in Apple’s app picker, iOS gives Locturne opaque tokens. Locturne cannot turn a token into an app name or icon; only iOS can, and it draws them on screen for you. We never learn which apps, categories or websites you chose.

For daily time limits, iOS tells Locturne’s extension when a limit you set has been reached, so the app can be put to sleep. Locturne doesn’t read your usage history or how long you spend in any app.

No Screen Time data ever leaves your iPhone. It isn’t sent to us or to any service provider, isn’t used for advertising or analytics, and is never sold or shared. Locturne’s Screen Time extensions contain no analytics and make no network requests.

You can turn off Locturne’s Screen Time access at any time in iOS Settings, under Screen Time. Locturne will tell you plainly that it can no longer block anything.

4. What leaves your iPhone

RevenueCat and PostHog are being added to Locturne before its public release. This section describes how they work in the released app. Early test builds may not include them.

Purchases: Apple and RevenueCat

Subscriptions are sold and billed by Apple through the App Store. We never see your payment card or Apple ID details. We use RevenueCat to check whether your subscription is active, restore purchases and choose which introductory offer you see. RevenueCat receives your App Store purchase and subscription history (products, dates, prices, trial status), an anonymous ID it creates for this app install, your country and currency from the App Store, and basic device and app details (model, iOS version, app version).

Product analytics: PostHog

We use PostHog to learn whether Locturne actually works for people: where setup gets confusing, and whether blocks start and mornings unlock. Analytics run in the main app only, never in the Screen Time extensions. PostHog receives:

  • events such as which setup screens you saw, whether you allowed Screen Time access, which wake-up method you chose, whether a trial started, whether a block started, when a morning unlocked, and passes or emergency unlocks used;
  • some of your setup answers, such as how you heard about Locturne, and an age range (for example 18–24), never your exact age. If you tell us you’re under 13, nothing more is sent;
  • the number of apps you picked, never which ones;
  • error reports;
  • a random ID for this install, your device model, iOS version, app version and language, and an approximate location (country or region) that PostHog works out from your IP address.

PostHog doesn’t receive your name, email, contacts, location from GPS, Health data, or anything about the apps you block. It isn’t used for advertising. PostHog stores this data in the United States. It uses your IP address only to work out the approximate location, then discards it.

Legal bases (EEA and UK)

We process data to provide the app and your subscription (performance of a contract), to understand and fix how the app works (our legitimate interest in a working product, balanced against your privacy by keeping analytics free of app selections and personal details), and to email the waitlist (your consent, which you can withdraw at any time). Our service providers are based in the United States, so your data may be processed there under the safeguards those providers offer, such as Standard Contractual Clauses.

5. The website and waitlist

locturne.com is hosted by Cloudflare. Like any web host, Cloudflare processes your IP address and browser details to deliver the site and protect it from abuse. The site sets no cookies and loads no ads or trackers.

If you join the waitlist, we store:

  • your email address;
  • the tag on the link you followed, if there was one (for example which video you came from: ?src= and utm_ tags);
  • the name of the site that sent you, such as tiktok.com (not the full address);
  • your country, as Cloudflare reports it; and
  • the date you signed up.

We don’t store your IP address with your sign-up. The page remembers the link tag in your browser’s session storage until you close the tab, so it isn’t lost if you open another page first.

We use your email only to tell you about the Locturne beta and launch, and the link tags only to count which videos bring sign-ups. We don’t sell the list or share it for anyone else’s marketing. 7. How long we keep data

  • On your iPhone: until you delete Locturne. Deleting the app removes its data, including the App Group.
  • RevenueCat: purchase records are kept while you subscribe and afterwards as needed for tax, accounting and fraud prevention.
  • PostHog: analytics events are kept for up to 12 months, then deleted.
  • Waitlist: until you unsubscribe or ask us to delete it, or until we stop using the list.

8. Your choices and rights

  • Screen Time, Motion & Fitness, Camera and Notifications can each be turned off in iOS Settings. Locturne keeps working as far as it can and tells you what it can no longer do.
  • Delete the app to erase everything Locturne stored on your iPhone. A reinstall starts with a new random analytics ID. Deleting the app doesn’t cancel a subscription; see the Terms.
  • Ask us to access, correct, delete or export data we or our providers hold about you, or to object to its use. Email hello@locturne.com. We’ll reply within 30 days and won’t treat you differently for asking.

Depending on where you live (for example the EEA, the UK, California or other US states), you may have these rights by law, and you can complain to your local data protection authority. Because we don’t sell or share personal information for advertising, there is nothing to opt out of; if that ever changed, we would update this policy and give you a way to opt out first.

9. Children and teens

Locturne is not directed to children under 13, and our Terms require users to be at least 13. We don’t knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 has given us personal information (for example by joining the waitlist), email us and we will delete it.

Teens aged 13 to 17 need a parent or guardian’s permission to use Locturne. On a child account, Apple’s Ask to Buy sends purchases to a parent to approve. If iOS gives Locturne an age range, it’s used on your iPhone to apply the rules for that age and isn’t sent to us.

10. Security

Data on your iPhone is protected by iOS, in storage only Locturne and its extensions can read. Data sent to RevenueCat, PostHog and the website is encrypted in transit (HTTPS). Access to the waitlist is limited to the people who run Locturne. No system is perfectly secure; if a breach affected your data, we would tell you as the law requires.
