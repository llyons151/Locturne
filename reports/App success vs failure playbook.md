# Winners plan distribution before they build

Researched and written October 9, 2026, from four research notes in `research_notes/App success vs failure playbook/`, checked against [GAME_PLAN.md](../GAME_PLAN.md), [docs/EDGE_10K.md](../docs/EDGE_10K.md), [docs/2K_MRR_RESEARCH.md](../docs/2K_MRR_RESEARCH.md), [docs/LAUNCH_PLAN.md](../docs/LAUNCH_PLAN.md) and [docs/TODO.md](../docs/TODO.md).

The developers who repeatedly ship subscription apps that earn money don't write better code than the ones who fail. They choose a problem people already pay to solve. They add one new mechanic you can understand from two seconds of video. They plan and test distribution before or alongside the build, post a lot of content and kill weak hooks quickly, then put most of their in-app effort into the onboarding paywall, because that one screen decides most of the money on install day. Failed indie apps mostly fail at the first step: in the post-mortems collected here, about six of every eight cite "nobody found it" rather than a bad product. The base rates are harsh. Only about **17% of new subscription apps reach $1K a month within two years**, and the median app earns about **$72 a month** after its first year, so the success stories below are survivors and shouldn't be read as typical results. Locturne already follows much of the playbook: a proven category, a filmable mechanic (walking downstairs), a hard paywall with a 7-day trial, a founder who can get views, and a January launch in the strongest month for Health & Fitness. Its biggest gaps are calendar items with near deadlines, not strategy: the overnight device proof, the App Store Connect setup, and an Apple featuring nomination that has to go in by the end of October. Its biggest unknown is whether views turn into buyers. Rivaldle and Soulsdoku showed that views alone don't.

## Five in six new subscription apps never reach $1K a month

Start with what usually happens, because every success story sits on top of it. RevenueCat's 2026 report covers more than 115,000 apps. It finds that **17.3% of new apps reach $1K in monthly revenue within two years and 4.6% reach $10K**. Both shares are lower than a year earlier. At one year after launch, the median app earns about $72 a month, the top quarter earns more than $429, and the top 10% earns more than $2,574 ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps); [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/the-app-middle-class-is-dying-and-revenuecats-data-shows-exactly-how-fast/)). The money is concentrated at the top. Adapty finds the **top 10% of apps capture 94.5% of subscription revenue** ([Adapty SOIS 2026](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions/)). The field is also getting more crowded: new subscription apps per month went from about 2,000 in January 2022 to more than 14,700 in January 2026, while apps launched before 2020 still earn 69% of subscription revenue ([Subscription Insider](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/article-type/news/revenuecat-data-shows-subscription-app-growth-concentrating-at-the-top)).

Even these numbers flatter the typical app. RevenueCat and Adapty only see apps that installed their subscription SDKs. Those apps already intend to charge, and many are run by teams who test paywalls. Apps that ship and earn nothing are mostly missing from the data, so the true base rate is worse. Both companies also sell paywall tooling, which colours claims like Adapty's "apps that run experiments earn 40x more". That figure is a correlation: bigger apps can afford to run tests.

One finding helps more than the rest. **Apps that do reach $1K get there fast, in a median of 58 days**, and reach $10K in a median of 109 ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)). An app that is nowhere near $1K four to six months after a real distribution push is statistically unlikely to get there without a major change. That makes a checkpoint at around 60–90 days meaningful rather than arbitrary.

Category matters too, and it favours Locturne. RevenueCat's 2024 report found Health & Fitness apps performing at least twice as well as other categories after a year ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2024/03/13/mobile-app-revenue/)). Adapty finds H&F has the highest median one-year install value ($1.21), just ahead of Productivity ([Adapty H&F benchmarks](https://adapty.io/blog/health-fitness-app-subscription-benchmarks/)). The plan to list Locturne in Health & Fitness rather than Productivity is supported by this data.

### Why apps fail, in the order post-mortems cite it

The failure write-ups collected here fall into a clear pattern. The most common cause by far is **launching to no audience**. In one case, a year of part-time work on a game sold nothing in two months because the developer "hoped the App Store would do the job" ([kwcodes](https://dev.to/kwcodes/how-i-failed-5-side-projects-in-6-years-earning-0-3j19)). In another, a developer of several "high-quality" tools got fewer than five users, zero engagement from ten days of build-in-public posts, and a Reddit ban for self-promotion ([xor01](https://xor01.substack.com/p/building-isnt-enough-a-developers)). A 41-app portfolio went 37 days without a sale because every app was paid upfront with no free entry ([phinnshen](https://dev.to/phinnshen/37-days-of-zero-app-store-sales-i-pulled-the-data-on-all-41-of-my-apps-2kl2)). The second most common cause is **building without testing demand**, often by making something "better, not different". One founder's products "never made it past 20 users" because users asked "why not just use Notion?" ([Andres Max](https://andresmax.substack.com/p/lessons-i-learned-from-my-failed)).

After those two come conversion and structure problems: no free entry point, a long wait before the user sees the value, and pricing that signals low quality. Rarer causes include retention collapsing after a burst of attention, a market too small to sustain the app, and platform shocks. Retention collapse is best shown by Poparazzi, which went from #1 on the App Store and about 4M monthly users to about 2,000 before shutting down ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/05/01/once-hot-photo-sharing-social-app-poparazzi-is-shutting-down/)). Platform shocks include Reddit's API pricing killing Apollo ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_(app))) and App Review standoffs like Hey's ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=23542937)). The last cause is giving up before results compound. HabitKit's founder went back to a job in 2023 because income "wasn't enough to live off of", then reached $10K a month in 2024 with the later app ([Sebastian Röhl](https://sebastianroehl.substack.com/p/2024-my-indie-app-business-year-in)).

The order matters for planning. For a solo developer, distribution is the first bottleneck. Retention only becomes the bottleneck once a channel works. One developer put it this way: when any factor is zero, the result is zero. This sample has its own survivorship bias in reverse. Founders whose app found users but couldn't keep them write fewer post-mortems, so "no retention" is probably under-counted.

## Winners pick proven demand plus one mechanic you can film

The best-documented repeat winner is Blake Anderson, with RizzGPT, Umax and Cal AI. In his own words, he went "from idea to execution 3x on this playbook in one year" ([Anderson on X](https://x.com/blakeandersonw/status/1842349939088925146)). Forbes put the three apps together at $15.4M in annual run rate (ARR), all bootstrapped ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/josipamajic/2024/10/07/hacking-the-app-store-gen-zs-15m-arr-bootstrapped-success-story/)). Fortune later reported Cal AI alone at about $30M a year, but TechCrunch **could not verify** the revenue claims ([Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2025/09/27/gen-z-founder-treats-college-like-vacation-30-million-app-by-18); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/03/teen-with-4-0-gpa-who-built-the-viral-cal-ai-app-was-rejected-by-15-top-universities)). Anderson's playbook is explicit about choosing ideas. A niche "heavily monetized with physical products" shows that people in it already pay. He plans the content and channels **before building** and validates through content before writing code ([BetterLaunch](https://www.betterlaunch.co/playbooks/episodes/blake-3apps)). He also warns that straight copycat apps, even with strong distribution, top out around $10K–$100K a month ([BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/fcd271f2cf563146)).

Nobody else in the research has a three-for-three record at that level. The other documented repeat earners use a different model: many cheap apps chosen by App Store keyword data, each making a little. Adam Lyttle reports 50+ simple apps earning $50–60K a month together ([Jawbreaker](https://jawbreaker.transistor.fm/episodes/from-zero-to-hero-how-adam-lyttle-made-800000-on-the-app-store-with-simple-apps)). Locturne is not that kind of app. It is one product that needs a viral moment, so Anderson's model fits it better.

The pattern behind these examples is **a proven category plus one new mechanic you can show on camera**. Cal AI took calorie counting, which people already pay for, and added photo-to-calories, which shows up instantly on video. The anti-doomscrolling category has produced several separate winners since 2022. Opal reports $1M ARR within four months with no paid ads ([Speedinvest](https://speedinvest.com/blog/scaling-smart-how-opal-built-a-10m-arr-business-in-just-2-years)). One sec grew for months off a single screen-recording tweet ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/frederik-riedel-expected-12-his-app-cut-screen-time-by-57)). Brainrot made $26K in its first 30 days ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/this-chronic-side-hustler-just-hit-26k-in-30-days-fYbp8x4WWqltdifUwCZJ)). Each grew from one organic viral moment and then turned it into a repeatable content routine. Several of these founders built the app for their own phone habit first. **Locturne fits the pattern**. App blockers and morning-task alarms are both proven paid categories, and "my apps stay asleep until I walk downstairs" can be filmed in one shot. One caveat from the earlier research applies: [EDGE_10K](../docs/EDGE_10K.md) found the narrow "unlock after a morning proof" niche crowded and still unproven, with the best verified competitor at about $469 MRR. The money sits in the neighbouring markets: scrolling in bed (Opal) and failing to get up (Erly, Wayk). The videos should speak to those bigger pains.

### Waitlists predict less than founders hope

Evidence that pre-launch validation predicts revenue is thin and mostly comes from vendors. The figure people quote most is **5–25% of a waitlist converting to paid**. It's about 20% if people are contacted within a month of signing up and under 10% after three months. The data comes from Lenny Rachitsky in 2022 and leans toward B2B products ([speedrun](https://speedrun.substack.com/p/the-growth-meta-how-to-build-a-waitlist)). A real indie case came in far lower: **300+ waitlist sign-ups produced 3 paying users**, about 1%, which the founders blamed on a long wait before users saw value ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/300-waitlist-signups-launched-march-12-and-only-3-paying-users-where-is-our-gtm-failing-808aec814d)). In the other direction, a landing page plus three months of Google Ads that produced only five sign-ups correctly killed a project before it was built ([kwcodes](https://dev.to/kwcodes/how-i-failed-5-side-projects-in-6-years-earning-0-3j19)). A waitlist can rule an idea out more reliably than it can predict revenue.

Apply that to the November 29 gate (≥3 sign-ups per 1K views and ≥300 sign-ups). With 300 sign-ups and the realistic 1–10% range, expect roughly **3 to 30 paying customers from the list at launch**. That's useful, but it isn't a business. The gate's real value is the per-hook rate, because it tells you which hook brings in people who act. Two adjustments follow from the evidence. First, the list will be five to eight weeks old by January 4, and conversion decays with age, so email the list in December with real footage and again on launch day. Second, App Store **pre-orders** are the strongest native signal. They download automatically on release, and App Store Connect reports them as placed, cancelled and fulfilled ([Apptamin](https://www.apptamin.com/blog/apple-pre-order-app/)). Once a build is approved, moving waitlist traffic to a pre-order page turns stated interest into an install with no extra step.

## Distribution is volume and patience, not one perfect video

Two low-cost video models are documented. In the first, Anderson **paid two small creators $50 each and reports 5–10M views overnight**, about 45K downloads on day one and about 200K that week. He also says a common mistake is messaging around ten influencers, getting silence, and concluding that "marketing is hard" ([BetterLaunch](https://www.betterlaunch.co/playbooks/episodes/blake-3apps); [Kickstart](https://kickstartsidehustle.com/how-to-make-80k-mrr-within-the-first-month-on-your-app-3-times-demo/)). These figures are self-reported and come from someone who now sells the playbook. In the second, BePresent, a screen-time app at about $20K MRR, runs seven TikTok and two Instagram accounts. Its founder and brand slideshows reached 8.9M views on a single post, while **identical cover photos produced anywhere from 0 to 2.9M views**. Its paid creator account managed only 44K views in a month ([Social Growth Engineers](https://www.socialgrowthengineers.com/productivity-app-tiktok-ig-strategy-to-20k-mrr-40k-installs)).

UGC agency data shows the same high variance: most week-one videos average about 1K views, the first video over 10K arrives in week two, and two or three breakouts appear by week three ([The Playbook by Suzanna](https://theplaybookbysuzanna.substack.com/p/how-150-creators-and-600-tiktoks)). This is why volume beats polish. Breakouts are rare and unpredictable, so the chance of a hit grows with the number of hooks and formats you post, not with how good any one video is. Kill hooks weekly and keep the app.

Authenticity matters in this category. Opal hired a TikTok creator who was already an organic user instead of running a brand account ([BetterLaunch / Sub Club](https://www.betterlaunch.co/playbooks/episodes/kenneth-schlenker-opal-billion-user)). BePresent's owned accounts beat its paid creator account. Both point the same way as the decision already in [EDGE_10K](../docs/EDGE_10K.md): founder-led, disclosed accounts, no faceless farms.

Conversion from views to installs is almost never published, which is the weakest link in the whole playbook. One self-reported "phone farm" figure is **about 2,000 views per install with 5% of installs paying** ([Sulfur on X](https://x.com/sulfurscales/status/2083953297333870914?lang=en)). It also carries platform-policy risk. At that rate, 1M views is about 500 installs. At Locturne's target hard-paywall conversion of around 10%, that's about 50 annual subscribers, or roughly $2,500 of first-year cash after Apple's 15% cut. Treat that as a floor, not a forecast. The [EDGE_10K](../docs/EDGE_10K.md) research found installs per 1K views ranging more than 1,000-fold between apps. The difference came from whether the viewer has the problem, whether the app is the payoff on screen, and whether there is a visible next step. That's why Rivaldle's and Soulsdoku's million-view videos aren't evidence that Locturne will sell. The hook lab has to measure buyers ("what app is this?" comments and sign-ups per 1K views), not views.

## The paywall decides most of the money on day zero

Hard paywalls are the strongest monetization choice the data supports. By day 35, **a median 10.7% of downloads pay on a hard paywall versus 2.1% on freemium**. Revenue per install at day 60 is $3.09 versus $0.38. One-year retention of people who did pay is almost the same, 27% versus 28% ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps); [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/the-app-middle-class-is-dying-and-revenuecats-data-shows-exactly-how-fast/)). The costs are somewhat higher refunds (5.8% versus 3.4%) and fewer free users to spread the word ([RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025/)). Opal shows that trade-off from the other side: moving to freemium cut its paid conversion from about 20% to 9%, which it accepted in exchange for word of mouth ([BetterLaunch / Sub Club](https://www.betterlaunch.co/playbooks/episodes/kenneth-schlenker-opal-billion-user)). All of these comparisons are between different apps, not controlled tests, and apps that suit a hard paywall tend to choose one. For a student who needs cash soon, the hard paywall is still the right default, and keeping a freemium fallback ready, as GAME_PLAN does, is sensible.

Timing explains why onboarding matters so much. **Between 82% and 90% of trials start on install day**, and about half of all paid conversions happen on day 0 ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps); [Adapty SOIS 2026](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions/)). In practice the onboarding paywall is the main conversion event, which is why Anderson and Lyttle both name paywall testing as their biggest post-launch lever. Lyttle says one paywall change "nearly 3x'd" revenue ([Jawbreaker](https://jawbreaker.transistor.fm/episodes/unlocking-indie-app-success-the-game-changing-tools-behind-adam-lyttles-revenue-surge)). Superwall's data from about 40M paywall opens shows multi-page onboarding paywalls converting **37% better** than single-page ones. That data is observational and comes from a vendor with no category controls ([Superwall](https://superwall.com/blog/new-postmulti-page-onboarding-paywalls-convert-37-better-than-single-page-heres-why.md)). No rigorous evidence shows that long quizzes help on their own. Locturne's two-page paywall after a dated trial timeline fits the best-supported pattern. The open question is whether 22 screens is too many, and only funnel data will answer it.

Trial length and price both trade conversion against value. Trials of 17–32 days convert **42.5%** of trials to paid, versus 37.4% for 5–9 days and 25.5% for 4 days or less. A 7-day trial sees 39.8% of its cancellations on day 0, compared with 55.4% for a 3-day trial ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps); [SOSA 2026 part 2](https://www.revenuecat.com/sosa-2026-insights-part-2)). Locturne's 7-day trial sits in the sensible middle. Testing a 14-day trial, already one of the exit-offer arms, is backed by this data. On price, high-priced apps earn far more per payer: year-one value of $62.19 versus $10.69 for low-priced apps, though one-year retention is lower (23% versus 36%) ([ppc.land](https://ppc.land/the-app-middle-class-is-dying-and-revenuecats-data-shows-exactly-how-fast/)). Health & Fitness is the one category where annual plans still dominate, at about 61% of revenue ([Adapty SOIS 2026](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions/)).

At $59.99, Locturne is well above the median yearly price of about $35, which suits an annual-first H&F app. Anderson's counterexample is real: he priced Cal AI low on purpose to widen the funnel ([Kickstart](https://kickstartsidehustle.com/how-to-make-80k-mrr-within-the-first-month-on-your-app-3-times-demo/)). But he had a mass-market product and a creator budget, so it doesn't override the LTV data for a niche app. For your cash needs, the most important point is that **annual plans are charged upfront**. With a 7-day trial, most of the money from an install arrives within about eight days, so cash tracks installs almost directly.

## January is the strongest window, but the launch is 100 days long

The App Store store page converts on its first impression, not its description. **Only 9–17% of visitors scroll through all the screenshots** ([PICC](https://explore.picc.co/app-store-screenshots-costing-downloads/); [ButterKit](https://www.butterkit.app/guides/app-store-design-cheatsheet/)), and on iOS the first three portrait screenshots also appear in search results ([AppFollow](https://appfollow.io/blog/aso-screenshots-best-practices)). For H&F apps, an iOS product page converts somewhere between 15% and 31% of visitors, depending on the source and how the funnel is defined ([Adjust citing AppTweak](https://www.adjust.com/blog/what-is-a-good-conversion-rate); [Sonar](https://trysonar.app/blog/app-store-conversion-rate)). That makes the icon, title and screenshots 1–3 Locturne's most important ASO assets. The common belief in a new-app "honeymoon" ranking boost rests on a 2014 post with two examples ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/why-app-store-keyword-rankings-drop-dramatically-seven-days-after-launch)). Developers also reported that new apps "barely ranked" in search for about a year before WWDC 2026 ([PricePush](https://pricepush.app/blog/app-store-rankings-2026)). Don't count on search to carry the launch. Custom Product Pages, which let one app have several store pages for different traffic sources, give single-digit average lifts (3–7%) ([AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/guide-to-custom-product-pages-cpp)). Their real value is as a separate landing page for each video hook, so you can see which hook sells.

Apple featuring is the cheapest large upside available. Featuring Nominations in App Store Connect include an **App Launch** type that also covers pre-orders. Apple asks for at least 2–3 weeks' notice and recommends up to three months ahead "for wider consideration". You can attach an In-App Event and a public TestFlight link ([App Store Connect Help](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-featuring-nominations/nominate-your-app-for-featuring); [Apple, Getting featured](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/getting-featured/)). The uplift data is old. In 2017–18, App of the Day raised median US weekly downloads by about 685%, and the gain was much larger for little-known apps than for apps already in their category's top 20 ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2018/04/20/ios-11-app-store-developers-are-happy/)). Treat a feature as a short burst to turn into ratings and retained subscribers, not as a growth plan. Apple says it values innovation, "a fresh approach to a familiar category", UI quality and accessibility. That lines up with the barometer mechanic and the native SwiftUI direction.

Seasonality favours a January launch. **January 2025 set an all-time Health & Fitness record of $385M in in-app revenue**, up 10% year over year ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/state-of-mobile-health-and-fitness-in-2025)). January has consistently been the category's top month for downloads ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/health-and-fitness-app-record-download-growth)), with installs for individual apps peaking in the first week. Evidence on the best day of the week is old and weak. The most consistent advice is to avoid Friday, and weekday features have beaten weekend ones ([TechCrunch 2011](https://techcrunch.com/2011/12/19/sunday-is-the-best-day-to-launch-your-mobile-app); [MacRumors / Apptopia](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/app-of-the-day-major-download-boost.2080026/)). January 1, 2027 is a Friday, so release on **Monday January 4 or Tuesday January 5**. Expect a resolution-driven January cohort to churn faster, and measure it separately from later cohorts.

Don't over-plan launch day itself. A useful frame is about 100 days in total: roughly 45 before, the day itself, and up to 60 after ([Kickstart launch checklist](https://www.kickstart.tools/blog/the-ultimate-indie-ios-app-launch-checklist)). RevenueCat's David Barnard argues against betting on one big launch after his own failed one ([Sub Club](https://www.revenuecat.com/subclub)). Stacking channels within 48 hours costs little, but expect Product Hunt and Reddit to bring in tens of installs, not hundreds. The indie app Slopes got a few dozen from each, while one press mention brought 164 in a day ([Slopes Diaries](https://blog.curtisherbert.com/slopes-diaries-7-so-hows-that-going-for-you/)). The main benefit of a concentrated burst is early ratings and fast data, not chart rank. Even #10 in a fitness subcategory took roughly 2,200 iOS downloads a day in 2022 ([AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/most-downloaded-fitness-and-workouts-apps)).

Ratings and Apple Ads finish the launch toolkit. Moving from 3 to 4 stars is linked to roughly **46–89% higher conversion**, though the sources are vendors who don't publish their methods ([NP Digital](https://neilpatel.com/marketing-stats/app-downloads-vs-ratings/); [Alchemer](https://www.alchemer.com/resources/blog/mobile-app-ratings-and-reviews-2022-benchmarks/)). iOS shows the review prompt at most three times a year. Apple also says it may not appear when called from a button tap, and screening users with a "do you like the app?" question first is a rejection risk ([Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/skstorereviewcontroller/requestreview(in:)); [vp0](https://vp0.com/blogs/apple-in-app-store-review-prompt-safely)). Locturne's prompt after the first successful morning unlock follows these rules.

Apple Ads for H&F cost about **$2.38–2.94 per install** in the US in 2026 ([SplitMetrics](https://www.splitmetrics.com/blog/apple-search-ads); [Sonar](https://trysonar.app/blog/apple-search-ads-cost)). At about $51 net per annual subscriber, that pays back about twice over at a 10.7% download-to-paid rate, breaks even at about 5%, and loses money at 2%. That arithmetic is why ads should wait until organic users have given you a real conversion rate.

## Renewal, refunds and Screen Time's own traps decide year two

The first renewal is the big filter. RevenueCat puts the median first annual renewal at **23–40% depending on category**. Adapty puts Health & Fitness first-renewal retention at 30.3%, the lowest of any category ([SOSA 2026 part 2](https://www.revenuecat.com/sosa-2026-insights-part-2); [Adapty H&F](https://adapty.io/blog/health-fitness-app-subscription-benchmarks/)). About **35% of all annual-plan cancellations happen in the first month**, which means the first weeks of habit-building decide renewal long before the renewal date ([SOSA 2026 part 2](https://www.revenuecat.com/sosa-2026-insights-part-2)). Plan for refunds of around 5% on a hard-paywall annual H&F plan; H&F ran at 4.71% and hard paywalls at 5.8% ([SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025/)). The definitions changed between RevenueCat's 2025 and 2026 reports, so the two sets of renewal numbers can't be compared directly.

App blockers carry five specific risks that generic playbooks miss. First, entitlement and review friction. Family Controls needs distribution approval for each bundle ID, and apps have been rejected under guideline 2.5.1 even after removing Screen Time code ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/822078); [Newly](https://newly.app/how-to/family-controls-entitlement)). Second, an unfixed bypass: since 2023, any user can turn off an app's Screen Time authorization in Settings using their own Face ID. Apple has never replied to the report ([Apple forums thread 727291](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/727291)). Retention therefore depends on motivation and design, not enforcement, and "unbypassable" claims are a liability. Third, an activation leak: in one shipped blocker, **35% of installs granted Screen Time access but never picked an app**, and that group retained far worse ([habitdoom](https://habitdoom.com/blog/shipping-familycontrols-ios)). That writer is a competitor, but the fix, disabling the picker's Done button until something is chosen, costs almost nothing. Fourth, billing anger. In one unverified review analysis, complaints about trials auto-converting at $99.99 made up 24% of Opal's 1–3 star reviews ([justuseapp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1497465230/opal-save-time-daily/reviews)). Locturne's dated trial timeline and day-5 reminder are the direct counter. Fifth, free competition from iOS Screen Time and the donation-funded ScreenZen ([ScreenZen](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1541027222)).

### What to measure in the first 90 days

Founders who iterate well decide their kill and continue thresholds before launch. The ones who drift keep giving it "one more month", which one portfolio-builder blog names as the hardest habit to break ([BuildMVPFast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/portfolio-app-strategy-multiple-apps-revenue-solo-founder-2026)). For a single-product founder, what gets killed quickly is hooks, formats and accounts, not the app. The thresholds below come from RevenueCat medians and are derived suggestions, not industry standards ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps); [SaaStr summary](https://saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-how-115000-mobile-apps-deliver-16b-in-revenue-whats-working-whats-quietly-killing-growth)).

| Metric | Weak (fix before buying traffic) | Around median | Strong (scale) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product page view → install | under ~20% | 25–31% (H&F) | 35%+ |
| Install → trial start | under ~5% | ~9% (higher-priced apps) | 12%+ |
| Trial → paid | under ~30% | ~38–40% (H&F) | 60%+ |
| Download → paid (hard paywall) | under ~5% | ~10.7% | 15%+ |
| First-morning unlock among trial starters | — | no published benchmark; set your own bar (2K_MRR proposes 70% within 3 days) | — |

Read paywall and onboarding tests at around day 14, because day 0 dominates. Read trial-to-paid and refunds at days 30–60, and decide on paid scaling at day 90.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "launch performance" should mean for Locturne. The base rates say the realistic first goal isn't a viral number. It's a measured funnel: a hook that brings sign-ups at 3+ per 1K views, a paywall near the 10% hard-paywall median, and most trial starters completing a first morning. Apps that earn tend to show it within about two months, so January and February will tell you quickly whether to scale, change the positioning, or fall back to freemium. Those decisions are only as good as the instrumentation and thresholds you set before launch.

The survivor stories (Cal AI, Opal, BePresent) are useful for their mechanics, not their revenue figures. Most of those figures are self-reported, unverified, and told by people now selling courses or tools. What carries over is the part that doesn't depend on luck. Choose proven demand. Make the app the payoff on screen. Post many hooks and kill the weak ones. Put most of your effort into the day-0 paywall. Don't promise a lock that iOS can't guarantee. Locturne's strategy already follows this. The risk now is execution timing: a nomination missed in October, or a lock that hasn't been proven overnight, would cost more than any strategic change could make up.
