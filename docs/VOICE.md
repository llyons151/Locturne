# Loc's voice

Started September 24, 2026. This is the voice bible: the rules every line of copy
follows, from the shield to the App Store listing. Personality background is in
[MASCOT_DIRECTION.md](MASCOT_DIRECTION.md). Why the voice matters so much is in
[BRAND_PRINCIPLES.md](BRAND_PRINCIPLES.md): v1 has no mascot art, so the writing
is the brand.

## Who is talking

Loc is a raccoon who is nocturnal by nature and exhausted by it. Your phone
is what keeps him up. When your apps sleep at bedtime, he finally gets to sleep.
The morning walk drags him out of bed. You're both trying to become morning
people, and neither of you is happy about it.

**In one line:** a burnt-out best friend who is on your side and complaining the
whole way.

## The five traits

### 1. Tired, so he's brief

- Most lines are under eight words. Fragments are fine.
- He ends lines with periods, never exclamation points.
- His questions often drop the question mark because he can't be bothered:
  "Why are we awake."
- Italics carry emphasis. Caps and emoji never do: "Fine. *Fine.*"

### 2. Deadpan, not zany

- He uses understatement and flat delivery. The joke is how little he reacts.
- No "OMG", no "bestie", no meme phrases that will date in a month, no puns
  in the main lines.

### 3. A diva about himself, on your side

- He complains about *his* suffering: being awake, sunlight, your phone's glow,
  mornings existing.
- The punchline lands on him or the situation, never on the user.
- He and the user are a team: "we" is fine. The apps are "they" (as in "So are
  they"), the sleepy co-conspirators.

### 4. Grudgingly fond

- Affection leaks out rarely and reluctantly, and the rarity is what makes it
  land: "Fine." "I'll allow it." "You did okay. Don't make it weird."
- He never gushes.

### 5. Clear when it matters

When something is broken or serious (Screen Time access revoked, protection
off, emergency unlock, a payment problem), clarity comes first. He states the
problem and the fix plainly, and he can add one small bit of himself afterward.
Never make a warning ambiguous for a joke.

## Never

- **Guilt or shame:** "You failed," "You broke your streak," "You've wasted 3 hours."
- **Threats:** no "or else," nothing that sounds like punishment.
- **Wellness-app words:** journey, habits, mindful, wellness, productivity,
  self-care, "screen time" as a moral idea (it's fine when naming the iOS setting).
- **Cheerleading:** "Great job!", "You've got this!", "Let's go!", confetti energy.
- **Science lectures:** no blue-light facts or sleep statistics in his mouth.
- **Being mean about the user's apps or interests.** He doesn't care what you
  scroll. He cares that it's keeping him awake.

## Say this, not that

| Moment | Not this | This |
|---|---|---|
| Bedtime | "Time to wind down! 🌙" | "Phone down. I'm not asking." |
| Blocked app opened | "This app is blocked." | "Shh. I'm sleeping. So are they." |
| Morning, 0 steps | "Let's get moving! 💪" | "No." |
| Unlock at 200 | "Congrats! Apps unlocked! 🎉" | "I'm up. Don't talk to me yet." |
| Emergency unlock | "Are you sure? This will break your streak." | "I'll allow it. This once. Maybe." |
| Access revoked | "Something went wrong." | "Screen Time access is off, so I can't block anything. Turn it back on in Settings. Until then I'm just a raccoon." |

## How the voice shifts through the day

| State | Energy | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| Night | Half-asleep, whispering, short | "Shh." "Go to bed." |
| Morning | Grumpy, reluctant, bargaining | "I can hear you walking. I'm ignoring it." |
| Day | Awake but dry, a little smug | "I'm awake. Technically." |
| Serious | Plain and clear, one small wink at most | See "Clear when it matters" |

## Writing process

1. Write lines from real moments: your own mornings, what you actually
   thought when the alarm went off.
2. Write in batches of 20 per moment. Keep the best one or two.
3. Read each line aloud in a flat, tired voice. If it needs an exclamation point
   to work, cut it.
4. Test the best ones in concept videos. Lines people quote in the comments stay.
5. Log keepers in the line bank below with the moment they belong to.
