# Locturne research and plan for 2000 MRR

Researched October 7, 2026. Budget: **under $500 of additional cash**, as requested. All prices and calculations are USD unless a storefront is identified. [GAME_PLAN.md](../GAME_PLAN.md) remains the product source of truth. This is a research and execution proposal, not a change to the app or its launch date.

**Locturne can plausibly reach $2,000 MRR, but nobody can guarantee it. With this budget, the most defensible route is reliable nightly blocking, a demonstrable morning unlock, founder content, and tightly targeted customer recruitment.** Cash buys a few experiments; founder work must supply most of the customers. Before scaling, prove that strangers will pay the current price and keep using the routine.

For $2,000 **gross** MRR, the current $59.99 annual plan requires **401 active paid subscribers**. A mix of 241 annual and 80 monthly subscribers produces **$2,004 gross MRR**. For $2,000 after a 15% store commission, annual-only requires **471**. None of these numbers represents $2,000 of monthly take-home profit.

Evidence labels throughout: **Fact** means a checked source or repository observation; **calculation** means arithmetic; **hypothesis** means something Locturne must test; **operating target** means a proposed decision threshold, not an industry benchmark. Competitor listings describe advertised behavior, not independently tested reliability. Case studies describe survivors and do not establish success probabilities.

## 1 The decisions that matter most

1. **Sell a specific situation:** being awake in bed and scrolling instead of getting up. Lead with the physical morning unlock; explain the scheduled bedtime block immediately afterward.
2. **Keep the current prices initially:** $59.99/year with a seven-day trial and $9.99/month without a trial. Price is unvalidated, but cutting it immediately increases the customer count required before you know the real objection.
3. **Use one founder account per chosen platform.** Begin with one main platform and republish original footage to the others when manageable. Build a repeatable demonstration, not a network of faceless accounts.
4. **Recruit 20–30 relevant adults for a structured beta.** Then test actual purchases by strangers on the production App Store. TestFlight purchases cannot establish willingness to pay.
5. **Reserve the $500.** Release it in small stages after the product and conversion evidence improve. No broad paid acquisition campaign belongs in the initial plan.
6. **Measure value during the trial:** a working first night and morning, followed by repeated protected routines. A paid entitlement alone is insufficient evidence that the product works.
7. **Use January as an opportunity, not a deadline that overrides reliability.** GAME_PLAN targets January 2–5, 2027 with February as a fallback. Start customer research and truthful build footage now.

## 2 What current evidence says about demand

**Fact:** Pew's September 2026 chart reports that 62% of US adults ages 18–29 say smartphone use hurts how much sleep they get, a lot or a little. The underlying survey ran May 26–June 1, 2026. This establishes reported pain, not willingness to pay or clinical effectiveness. [Pew age breakdown](https://www.pewresearch.org/chart/a-majority-of-adults-under-30-say-their-smartphone-use-takes-a-toll-on-their-sleep/).

**Hypothesis:** the best initial buyer is an adult iPhone user who scrolls in bed repeatedly, has a concrete morning consequence, and has already tried an easily overridden solution. Examples are a young professional late for work or a student repeatedly missing an early class. Paid blocker users are especially useful interviews because they have already spent money on the category.

The broad market is sufficient for a few hundred customers. The unanswered question is whether Locturne reaches a segment that wants its exact routine at $60/year. A large number of people saying they scroll too much cannot settle that question.

### Initial audience priorities

| Segment | Why test it | Main objection | Priority hypothesis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adults with morning scrolling and work consequences | A recurring problem with an obvious cost | Another subscription; fear of being locked out | First |
| College students with missed morning classes | Easy to demonstrate and recruit locally | Price; no staircase; inconsistent schedule | First in parallel if accessible |
| Existing paying blocker users | Strong evidence of category willingness to pay | Why add a second blocker? | Interview early |
| General productivity viewers | Large audience | Vague pain and weak purchase intent | Secondary |
| Parents seeking control over a child's phone | A different authorization and support problem | Product fit and trust | Outside initial acquisition focus |
| People seeking insomnia or ADHD treatment | High potential need, but a different claim | Expectation of clinical benefit | Do not sell treatment claims |

Choose the first segment from observed activation and paid purchases, not stereotypes. Stairs are a filming advantage, not a condition every customer must meet. Include one-floor homes and people needing an alternative in the beta.

**Recommended promise:** “Your distracting apps sleep at bedtime and wake up after you get out of bed.” The demonstration is blocked app → physical proof → usable app. Loc's voice makes that demonstration recognizable.

Explain the actual gate and humane exits. The defensible advantage is the whole experience, reliability, distribution, and accumulated customer trust; the sensor itself is easy to imitate.

## 5 What subscription benchmarks establish

**Fact:** RevenueCat's 2026 report lists median day-35 download-to-paid conversion of 10.7% for hard-paywall apps and 2.1% for freemium. It reports 17.3% reaching $1K monthly revenue within two years. First annual renewal medians range from 23–40% across categories, with Productivity at 23%. These are population observations, not Locturne forecasts or proof that a paywall causes better economics. [2026 report](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps).

**Inference:** use the hard paywall already in GAME_PLAN as the initial commercial test, but do not budget against 10.7% conversion. The downside scenario below starts at 1%; 3% is a working scenario, and 7% is a stronger result to earn. They are assumed **retained paid subscribers per install**, after the relevant trial matures and early refund/expiry losses are considered.

Do not multiply separately reported medians of several funnel stages and call the result the median funnel. Different apps, traffic, countries, and cohorts may drive each median. A conversion rate only means something when the denominator, cohort age, and paid outcome are defined.

### Useful case studies with limits

**Fact:** Opal's founder described a move from a hard paywall to freemium, lowering paid conversion from 20% to 9% while overall usage and revenue grew. This supports testing total economics and recommendation behavior, rather than maximizing conversion alone. It does not mean a new app should copy Opal's mature free tier. [Founder interview summary](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/kenneth-schlenker-sub-club-podcast-2026).

**Fact:** Coconote's founders described organic distribution through a team of part-time content creators and reported that trial extensions helped some users experience value. “No paid ads” did not mean no creator cost or labor. For Locturne, borrow the demonstrated product format and objection diagnosis, not its scale or outcome. [Founder interview summary](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/brett-zack-coconote-sub-club-podcast-2026).

**Fact:** Opal's customer case study identifies December–January as a strong acquisition period for its business. That supports preparing for the New Year; it does not establish Locturne's seasonal conversion uplift. [Opal case study](https://www.revenuecat.com/customers/opal).

## 6 Define the revenue target before optimizing

**Fact:** RevenueCat normalizes an annual price by dividing by 12. Active paid subscriptions can still count after auto-renew is disabled, until access expires. Trials and free promotional access must not be treated as equivalent to paid subscribers. [MRR calculation](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/dashboard-and-metrics/charts/monthly-recurring-revenue-mrr-chart).

**Fact:** Apple's Small Business Program provides a 15% commission for eligible enrolled developers. Enrollment and its effective date matter; being a small business does not automatically make every transaction subject to 15%. [Apple program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/).

### Customer counts at the current prices

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $59.99 annual only | $4.9992 | 401 | 471 |

| 75% annual and 25% monthly by subscriber count | $6.2469 expected average | About 321 | About 377 |

The mix row is approximate because real customers come in whole numbers. **241 annual + 80 monthly = $2,004.00 gross.** After a 15% commission that is approximately $1,703.40 before other deductions.

**400 annual subscribers produce $1,999.67**, slightly below a strict $2,000 target. Use 401 for the exact threshold, and an operating goal of 450 annual subscribers if you want room for losses. That buffer is a target, not a refund forecast.

Keep five separate numbers visible:

1. Normalized gross MRR from valid paid entitlements.
2. Normalized proceeds after store fees and relevant adjustments.
3. Transaction revenue collected in the period.
4. Settled money in the bank.
5. Contribution after acquisition, refunds, tools, and support costs; founder compensation and taxes still need separate treatment.

An annual subscription paid today supports service for a year. Do not spend the full proceeds as though they are one month's profit. Also track paid subscriptions set to cancel; their current MRR is real but their future renewal is at risk.

### Costs that are easy to miss

RevenueCat currently advertises free usage up to $2,500 in **monthly tracked revenue**, then 1% of tracked revenue. This is a transaction-revenue measure, not the MRR target. A concentrated annual launch can cross that threshold before normalized MRR reaches $2K. [Pricing](https://www.revenuecat.com/pricing).

PostHog advertises one million free product-analytics events per month. Keep replay off under the existing privacy approach, use billing limits, and check other products separately. [Pricing](https://posthog.com/product-analytics/pricing).

Apple says qualifying payments arrive within 45 days after the end of the relevant fiscal month. Earned money is not instantly available to fund creator deals. [Payment timing](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/getting-paid/overview-of-receiving-payments/).

Other costs include Developer Program renewal, EAS builds, domain/email, creative production, payment/foreign-exchange adjustments where applicable, and the founder's time. GAME_PLAN says the developer account already exists; the budget below treats existing equipment and previously paid costs as sunk.

## 8 Prove product value before scaling distribution

**Repository observation:** GAME_PLAN still requires real-device overnight and morning gates. The recorded DEVICE_SPIKE results establish compilation and manual blocking on October 2; they do not establish completed production reliability across several nights and devices. The app has substantial implementation, but a source file or simulator preview cannot settle those gates. [Recorded spike](DEVICE_SPIKE.md), [device script](v1-build/DEVICE_TEST_SCRIPT.md).

### Beta structure

Recruit 20–30 adults with the exact problem, across more than one phone model and supported iOS version. Include stairs, one-floor homes, irregular weekends, travel, notification refusal, and relevant accessibility needs. Ask them to use the app for 7–14 days with informed expectations and a direct way to report failures.

Run the existing device script first. Then record, per scheduled night:

- Whether the chosen apps actually became blocked with Locturne closed.
- Whether the block held across relevant schedule boundaries.
- Whether each supported proof worked and released only the intended rules.
- Whether always-blocked apps retained their rule after a morning unlock.
- Whether passes and emergency exits behaved as documented.
- Whether revocation and failed protection produced honest status.
- Whether the user felt the result was worth continuing.

Ask for a short manual morning diary, with explicit consent, rather than expanding analytics to transmit bedtime, selected apps, altitude, or step counts. Existing privacy rules matter. A “night checked” event is useful but does not independently prove there was no bypass gap overnight.

### Proposed operating gates

| Gate | Target for deciding the next step | If it misses |
|---|---|---|
| Safety and honest status | No unresolved lockout, wrong-list unlock, or silent-protection failure | Repair and retest before public acquisition |
| Trial activation | At least 70% of eligible trial starters complete a first protected night and supported morning proof within three days | Investigate setup, permissions, schedule, and method friction |
| Repeated use | At least 50% of eligible beta/trial users complete three successful protected routines within seven days | Investigate annoyance, perceived value, and return-to-bed behavior |
| Commercial evidence | At least 10–20 unrelated production customers at the current price | Continue learning; do not declare the business validated |
| Refunding | Investigate every early refund; use 5% as a provisional alert after enough purchases to interpret a rate | Diagnose broken promises, technical failure, or unclear billing |

These are proposed thresholds, not sourced medians or guarantees. Nights off and users who have not yet had a scheduled night require appropriate denominators. Separate passes and emergency exits from successful physical proof without treating their use as moral failure.

Even 100 successful observed nights cannot establish perfect reliability. Failures may be correlated by iOS version or device. Keep release notes, diagnostics, and an incident process ready.

## 9 Customer research that tests payment rather than politeness

Conduct **15 initial interviews**, split between students and adults with work consequences; then concentrate on the segment showing the strongest behavior. This is a proposed sample for discovery, not a statistically representative survey.

Ask these before describing the app:

1. “Tell me about the last morning you were awake but stayed in bed scrolling.”
2. “What happened because of it?”
3. “How many mornings last week were like that?”
4. “What have you already tried? Show me, if you're comfortable.”
5. “Have you paid for any solution? Which one, and did you keep it?”
6. “Why did you stop using your last solution?”
7. “What must always remain available on your phone?”
8. “What would make this too annoying to use?”

Then show one truthful 15-second product demonstration and explain **$59.99 annually after the trial or $9.99 monthly**. Offer a beta slot or, once publicly available, the actual App Store purchase. Record what the person does. Do not interpret “I'd definitely buy that” as a sale.

At day seven, ask: “What changed in your morning?”, “When did you bypass or stop?”, “Did you get back into bed?”, “Would you keep it at the displayed price?”, and “Which person would you voluntarily recommend it to?” The last answer should be followed by a real referral only if the user wants to make one.

Maintain a table with segment, prior workaround, pain frequency, specific consequence, setup success, repeated use, production purchase, and objection. Keep personal details private and collect only what you need.

**Stop recruiting a segment for growth** if it enjoys the idea but repeatedly fails to use or buy it. Keep those findings; they can explain a pricing or product-fit problem.

## 10 Founder content as the main acquisition channel

### The format to test

Use a real phone and real footage. A useful 15–25-second sequence is:

1. **0–2 seconds:** open a chosen distracting app and show the shield. Hook: “My apps won't wake up until I get out of bed.”
2. **2–10 seconds:** show the proof in progress while physically moving. Use stairs when available; film a separate steps version for one-floor homes.
3. **10–15 seconds:** show the apps becoming available after successful proof.
4. **15–20 seconds:** explain that the selected apps block again at the chosen bedtime.
5. **Final seconds:** say and display “Locturne.” Before launch, point to the beta/waitlist; afterward, the App Store.

Humor should come from Loc and the situation. Keep testimonials truthful, obtain permission, and show essential access and exceptions when they answer a recurring concern. Do not film movement that is unsafe or pretend a mock screen is a functioning unlock.

### Eight original posts per week for the first six weeks

| Series | Example hook | What it tests |
|---|---|---|
| Bedtime-to-morning demo | “I can turn off my alarm in bed. I can't do this.” | Physical proof versus alarm-only framing |
| Downstairs demo | “My phone knows when I change floors.” | Curiosity about the actual sensor |
| Steps demo | “No stairs? This is the version I use.” | Reach beyond multi-floor homes |
| Previous failure | “I kept overriding my app limits at midnight.” | Recognition of the customer's current workaround |
| Seven-day personal diary | “Here's what changed after seven mornings.” | Sustained value, with clearly personal results |
| Objection reply | “What happens if I need an app urgently?” | Trust and emergency concerns |
| Loc's voice | Show the actual shield line and morning response | Recognition and brand preference |
| Founder development | “The overnight test failed. Here's what I'm fixing.” | Trust and useful build footage |

Create original variations of the best-performing demonstration. Republish clean footage to Reels and Shorts when time allows; track each separately. Eight posts per week is a workload target, not a claim about platform algorithms. There is no evidence here for a magic account-warming period, number of accounts per phone, or required posting hour.

### The measurement sheet

For each video record date, platform, hook, segment, method shown, duration, production minutes, views at 24 hours and seven days, profile visits, link visits where available, App Store page visits, attributed installs where available, self-reported source, and matured paid outcomes. Preserve unknown attribution rather than assigning all sales to the last video.

Use App Store campaign links or Custom Product Pages to compare message families. A web redirect records a click, not a guaranteed install or paid subscriber. Aggregate campaign results and an optional “how did you find us?” response can complement each other, but neither creates perfect user-level attribution. [Apple Custom Product Pages](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/custom-product-pages/).

Choose winners by **incremental paid revenue and retained routines relative to work**, not views alone. Early views and clicks help prioritize which format gets another attempt while paid cohorts mature.

### When content is failing

- **Low attention:** change the first two seconds, footage, or audience framing.
- **Views but no curiosity or visits:** make the problem and product identity clearer.
- **Visits but no installs:** inspect the store promise, credibility, compatibility, and pricing expectations.
- **Installs but no trial:** inspect the onboarding, permission friction, and paywall.
- **Trials but few first mornings:** repair product delivery before changing the hook.
- **First mornings but little payment or continued use:** investigate whether the problem is recurring and whether the routine is worth its annoyance and price.

After approximately **40 original posts and six weeks**, if no format generates identifiable qualified interest, change the segment or message rather than simply multiplying output. This is an operating checkpoint; it is not proof that the entire market has rejected the app.

## 11 Creators and communities within the budget

### What the budget rules out

Acquiring all 401 annual customers with $500 permits only **$1.25 cash CAC**; 321 mixed customers permits about **$1.56**. CAC means acquisition spending divided by acquired paying customers, not installs. Because this budget must also cover experiments and contingencies, actual paid-channel capacity is even smaller.

For comparison, an assumed $2 per thousand creator views, two installs per thousand views, and 3% paid conversion produces **$33.33 CAC** before any base fee. At 100,000 views, that is $200 spent, 200 installs, and six expected payers. The content may be economically useful later; it cannot buy the entire initial target on this budget.

### Small creator test after commercial proof

Spend at most **$150 on three small experiments** initially. A proposed offer is up to $50 for one usable original demonstration, with scope and rights agreed beforehand. These are budget caps, not researched market rates; many creators will decline. Work with smaller creators who already make believable morning content and want to test the product. Paying for creative production does not automatically include posting or reuse rights.

Choose based on audience fit, several recent videos, thoughtful comments, and ability to show the real app. Follower count alone is weak. Do not hire a sourcing platform or agency within this budget.

Agree on deliverable, posting date if applicable, raw-file/reposting permissions, disclosure, payment cap, and honest testimonial expectations. A useful result is either attributable buyers or a reusable format that works on the founder account; label those different outcomes.

An optional commission arrangement can be considered only when purchase attribution is practical. Pay from verified incremental paid outcomes after a refund observation period, exclude self-referrals, and cap obligations. Do not promise unlimited view bonuses or perpetual revenue share.

Material connections need clear disclosure under FTC guidance; TikTok also requires its commercial disclosure setting for relevant promotional content. [FTC disclosure guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers), [TikTok guidance](https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/creator-and-business-accounts/promoting-a-brand-product-or-service).

### Direct recruitment and partnerships

Use existing relationships, allowed community posts, and small targeted introductions to find interviewees and beta users. Once the App Store version exists, invite the same people to the real purchase only if the beta helped them. Free beta access does not count toward MRR.

Possible partners include a college study group, a morning-routine creator, or a productivity newsletter with an audience that already describes this problem. Pitch a useful demonstration and a small voluntary beta, rather than an unsupported productivity or health claim. Avoid school procurement and enterprise features before proving the consumer loop.

For communities, read the actual posting rules first. An allowed founder post should identify the creator, show the real product, state the current commercial status, and ask a focused question. Do not seed fake recommendations, buy reviews, or send bulk messages to people who did not ask. This report provides outreach drafts; it does not authorize sending messages.

### Outreach drafts

**Research invitation:** “I'm building Locturne for people who scroll in bed after waking up. I'm looking for adults who tried app limits and still override them. Could I ask about your last few mornings in a 15-minute conversation? I'm researching the problem before asking anyone to buy.”

**Creator invitation:** “I'm the maker of Locturne. It blocks chosen apps at bedtime and keeps them blocked until a physical morning check. Your [specific video] fits the problem well. I have up to $50 for one honest demo if the app works for you; posting and reuse rights would be agreed separately. Interested in trying it first?”

**Partner invitation:** “People in your group have mentioned scrolling through their morning. I'm recruiting a small adult beta for a bedtime-and-morning blocker. Could I share one demo and an optional sign-up in the format your group permits? It is a subscription product after launch; beta feedback is the goal.”

## 12 Store discovery and purchase conversion

Use the existing [listing](app-store/LISTING.md), [screenshots](app-store/SCREENSHOTS.md), and [owner checklist](app-store/OWNER_TODO.md) as the starting point. The exact title, subtitle, and keyword selection still need current storefront checks before submission. Initial phrases to investigate are “morning app blocker,” “stop scrolling in bed,” “bedtime app blocker,” and “get out of bed.” Broader “screen time” and “app blocker” searches may have intent but substantial competition. Make Locturne easy to find by its spoken name in demonstrations.

Every acquisition angle should match the store promise. A stairs video should reach a listing that makes physical morning unlocks obvious and shows the no-stairs alternative. Present the actual app, supported hardware, subscription terms, and emergency behavior. Do not promise an alarm or automatic background unlock unless the shipping build supports it.

Use honest testimonials only after you have them. Ask for App Store ratings through the permitted system flow following demonstrated value; never condition a reward on a positive review. Apple's guidelines govern truthful metadata, purchase information, and review practices. [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/).

At low volume, start with one clear store story and a small number of message families. A dozen Custom Product Pages creates work without enough data to choose among them. Apple supports approved Custom Product Pages for campaigns; keyword visibility has its own setup. [Configuration help](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/create-custom-product-pages/configure-multiple-product-page-versions).

A simple product page and a handful of useful search articles can support credibility. SEO is a slower supplementary channel; it is not a guaranteed first-quarter source of thousands of installs. Public content should answer real objections and show actual behavior rather than repeat broad screen-time advice.

## 13 Pricing and onboarding experiments

### Start with the existing commercial design

Keep the current annual/monthly offer, transparent billing amount, trial timeline, restoration, and subscription terms. Do not add weekly pricing or a lifetime plan simply to make a sales chart rise. Lifetime revenue cannot create subscription MRR.

The $59.99 annual price falls between Jomo and Opal's checked annual offers, but that does not validate it. Locturne has to justify its specific experience. An annual subscriber bought at $29.99 contributes half the recurring revenue of one bought at $59.99. With all other factors equal, a half-price offer requires roughly twice as many valid annual purchases to preserve recurring revenue per install.

Start by identifying the objection:

| Observed behavior | First investigation |
|---|---|
| People cannot explain the product before the paywall | Promise and demonstration |
| They understand it but abandon permissions | Trust, requirements, and setup |
| They have working trials but explicitly say the price is too high | Willingness to pay and a controlled price test |
| They cancel before experiencing any morning | Setup and time to value; a trial extension may help |
| They cancel after several successful mornings because it is annoying | Product fit and method settings |
| They buy but refund after a failed night | Reliability and truthful expectations |

The current roughly 22-screen onboarding is not inherently good or bad. Measure where users leave and whether each screen earns its place. Keep the schedule, chosen apps, supported proof, clear terms, and confirmation essential to tonight working. Extra quiz content needs a demonstrated reason to stay.

### Test order

1. Correct bugs, dead ends, unavailable methods, and unclear prices immediately.
2. Test a clearer opening promise or real demonstration.
3. Test removing an observed low-value onboarding interruption.
4. Test the existing exit offer only when enough eligible cohorts mature.
5. Test annual price or free access only after the prior evidence explains what it might solve.

GAME_PLAN already permits an exit-offer experiment. For limited traffic, propose focusing on **no offer versus the existing full-price 14-day trial**, deferring the half-price arm until there is enough data and a price objection. This is a proposal, not an implemented change. A 14-day user is still unpaid on day seven; account for the different maturity window.

Judge variants by day-35 revenue after refunds per eligible install, retained routines, and normalized MRR contribution. Trial starts are a leading signal, not the final objective. A longer trial could raise paid conversion while delaying cash or increasing support; a discount could raise sales and reduce MRR per install.

At a 3% conversion rate, 100 installs produce only about three payers. Small apparent differences are unstable. A conventional test seeking to distinguish 3% from 4% conversion can require on the order of **5,300 installs per arm**, assuming independent observations, 80% power, and a two-sided 5% significance level. That is a planning calculation, not a required universal sample. Low-volume founders should prioritize large observed problems and avoid claiming statistical wins from tiny tests.

### When a free tier deserves consideration

If users repeatedly succeed during trials, value the routine, and would recommend it, but few buy and organic sharing stays weak, investigate whether a meaningful free experience would increase total paid acquisition. GAME_PLAN already keeps this fallback open.

Designing that tier is a separate product decision. Compare paid contribution per install, cost to support free users, and referral behavior. A lower conversion percentage can still create more revenue if substantially more qualified people arrive and stay. Conversely, free use without referrals can absorb all the founder's capacity. Keep emergency access independent of monetization.

## 14 Retention determines whether 2K lasts

The first renewal is a second purchasing decision. Annual plans make first-year MRR look stable even when users stopped benefiting months ago. Watch repeated routines, permission revocation, support complaints, and subscriptions set to cancel before the renewal cliff arrives.

### Retention actions in priority order

1. Make tonight's protection real and its status honest.
2. Make the first morning proof succeed without surprise setup.
3. Make travel, nights off, illness, and no-stairs situations manageable using the current rules and exits.
4. Explain next-bedtime settings changes clearly so users do not mistake them for bugs.
5. Keep Loc recognizable without repetitive or shaming messages; collect which lines users actually enjoy.
6. Give an understandable record of successful routines, without inventing sleep improvements from shield events.
7. Answer lockout, billing, and failed-protection reports promptly; treat recurring complaints as product work.
8. Ask departing users why they stopped, optionally. Repair the reason before offering a discount.

Notifications should support the chosen routine and disclose trial billing as planned. More notifications are not automatically more retention. Follow existing permissions and privacy choices.

### Replacement arithmetic

Assume, purely for sensitivity, 400 active annual customers and a 30% first-year renewal rate. About **280 replacement annual purchases** are needed around the next year to restore that annual base, approximately 23.3/month when smoothed. At 3% retained paid conversion, that is roughly **778 installs/month** just to replace those annual losses, before monthly-plan churn or growth.

Actual losses arrive in purchase cohorts, not evenly. A January-heavy launch may create a January-heavy renewal drop next year. Build a schedule of expected renewals and the share set to cancel. Do not use smoothed replacement math as a cash forecast.

For 200 monthly customers at an assumed 8% monthly churn, replacing 16 customers/month holds the base flat. At 15%, replacing 30/month does. At 3% retained paid conversion those require about 534 and 1,000 installs/month respectively. The renewal rate has to be measured for Locturne before it informs lifetime value.

### Supporting people without exhausting the founder

Keep one support inbox, a clear emergency guide, known-issue notes, and a short diagnostic collection process with user consent. Prioritize wrong blocks, failure to unlock, billing confusion, and failed nighttime protection. Publish useful answers to repeated questions. Do not promise round-the-clock support you cannot provide.

Track minutes spent on support each week. For example, 400 customers averaging five minutes each per month would create about 33 hours of monthly support work. That is an assumption showing why simplicity and reliability affect profit, not a forecast of actual demand.

## 15 Analytics and a weekly operating dashboard

The repository already defines `onboarding_started`, `paywall_viewed`, `purchase_result`, `night_armed`, `morning_unlocked`, `night_checked`, passes, emergencies, and notification events. Use [ANALYTICS.md](ANALYTICS.md) and the existing code before adding more tracking. Configure and verify real production events; exclude development, sandbox, stub purchases, and free beta access.

An app-side `purchase_result = purchased` can indicate trial access. **Paid conversion must come from store/RevenueCat transaction lifecycle data**, with deduplication and correct product, cohort, refunds, and expiry. Restore events are not new customers.

| Weekly metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| First-time production installs by source where knowable | Size and quality of acquisition |
| Paywall reaches per install | Setup/onboarding loss |
| Eligible trial starts and direct monthly purchases | Leading commercial demand |
| First successful protected routine per eligible trial | Initial delivery of the promise |
| Three successful routines per eligible cohort in seven days | Repeated value |
| Matured trial-to-paid and install-to-paid | Real commercial conversion |
| Paid subscriptions by price and duration | Correct MRR and plan mix |
| Refunds, billing failures, expiries, and set-to-cancel share | Revenue quality |
| Gross MRR, estimated proceeds, collected revenue, bank cash | Different financial realities |
| Acquisition cash spent and founder hours | Cash risk and workload |
| Support incidents and time | Reliability and operating burden |

Break down by acquisition message, selected method, app version, and cohort age when sample sizes permit. Do not create dozens of tiny segments. Distinguish unknown outcomes from failures when a user does not reopen; absence of an analytics event does not prove absence of protection.

Maintain an experiment log: hypothesis, one change, eligible population, dates, guardrails, minimum observation period, result, and decision. Export enough history to inspect annual renewal cohorts next year. Do not expand into user-level selected apps or motion telemetry to get prettier dashboards.

## 16 Allocate less than 500 dollars

This is a **spending ceiling**, not a shopping list. Existing paid accounts and equipment are assumed. Revenue-dependent tool fees and eventual renewals are ongoing costs to fund from settled revenue; if they must fit inside the same absolute cap, reduce optional creator spend.

| Item | Maximum additional cash | Release condition |
|---|---:|---|
| Domain/email or necessary hosting gap | $25 | Only if existing setup cannot serve the beta and store support |
| Interview/beta incentives | $75 | Thank people for time; never require a purchase or positive review |
| Three creator experiments | $150 | Reliable demo and initial production purchase evidence |
| Essential builds or unavoidable tooling | $75 | Needed for device testing/release; use existing/free capacity first |
| Conditional acquisition experiment | $50 | A measured funnel and a narrow question worth spending on |
| Contingency held back | $100 | Critical release/support cost or a clearly successful experiment |
| **Total ceiling** | **$475** | **Keep at least $25 below $500** |

The $50 conditional acquisition line may be an Apple Ads test, but **$50 may be too little to establish a reliable paid CAC**. It can answer a narrow click/install question; it cannot prove scale. If you cannot define the question and attribution, keep the money. Do not put $10 on each of five ad channels.

Founder time is the largest investment. A proposed weekly allocation while preparing release is 6–8 hours of customer/distribution work, alongside engineering. After release, aim for about 10–15 hours/week for content, customer conversations, analysis, and support if feasible. If less time is available, reduce output expectations and extend the timeline; spending constraints do not make labor free.

Do not buy analytics upgrades, keyword tools, automation farms, mascot merchandise, or an influencer subscription to avoid the hard work of customer learning. Add a tool only when it removes a demonstrated bottleneck.

## 17 Acquisition economics before any reinvestment

For a full-price annual purchase, a simplified first-term proceeds ceiling is **$59.99 × 0.85 = $50.99**, before refunds, applicable taxes/adjustments, tool fees, and support. An illustrative 5% refund haircut brings this to about **$48.44**. This is a model, not Apple's actual settlement calculation.

An initial paid acquisition target of **$10–15 per valid annual customer** would leave substantially more room than spending the whole first-year proceeds. It is an operating target, and the initial $500 budget still cannot buy hundreds of customers even at that CAC.

| Assumed valid paid conversion per install | Maximum CPI at $15 CAC |
|---|---:|
| 1% | $0.15 |
| 3% | $0.45 |
| 7% | $1.05 |

CPI is cost per install. If a measured campaign costs $2/install and retained paid conversion is 3%, its CAC is about **$66.67**. That exceeds the simplified first-term annual proceeds above. An inexpensive-looking install is not necessarily an inexpensive customer.

Do not count speculative future annual renewals to excuse a losing channel. Add observed renewals to a lifetime-value model only when those cohorts exist. For monthly plans, use actual paid-month survival; revenue from one $9.99 payment does not justify annual-plan CAC.

If organic purchases produce settled cash, propose reserving **10–20% of those proceeds for small reinvestment tests**, with the rest covering service obligations, contingencies, and other business costs. This is optional and would expand beyond the user's initial budget if adopted; no extra spend is assumed or authorized here. Avoid debt or revenue advances to meet an arbitrary MRR date.

After the first few production cohorts, substitute observed installs, conversion, plan mix, and churn into the model. A forecast built from that data is more useful than an optimistic launch calendar.

## 19 Failure diagnosis and decisions

| Finding | Likely issue to investigate | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| Interviews show occasional annoyance but little consequence | Weak pain in the chosen segment | Recruit people with a repeated concrete cost |
| Beta users want it but blocking is unreliable | Promise delivery | Pause acquisition scaling and fix the engine |
| It works, but most users stop after two mornings | Habit friction or insufficient value | Interview departures; compare supported methods |
| Videos get substantial views but few qualified visits | Entertainment without demand | Show the use case and outcome sooner |
| Qualified visits produce few installs | Store fit or credibility | Align the listing and answer objections |
| Installs produce few trial starts | Onboarding, trust, or price | Observe sessions with consent and repair the largest loss |
| Trials activate but rarely become paid | Value/price mismatch | Diagnose objections; controlled offer test |
| Sales occur but refunds spike | Broken expectations or reliability | Fix the promise/product before spending |
| Organic works but a creator does not | Audience or acquisition economics | End that deal; reproduce the successful founder format |
| Annual MRR grows while routine use collapses | Renewal risk hidden by prepaid terms | Fix sustained value before expanding spend |

**Stop discretionary spending** after a reproducible serious protection/exit failure, unclear commercial tracking, or a test exceeding its cash cap. Keep necessary repair work going. “Stop spending” is not “abandon the project.”

After 1,000 reasonably targeted, matured installs, fewer than about ten valid payers would put the observed conversion below 1%. First verify tracking and traffic quality; then substantially revise the message, onboarding, audience, or offer before trying to purchase more traffic. This is a proposed decision checkpoint, not a universal definition of product-market fit.

If customers reliably use and pay but installs remain scarce, distribution is the bottleneck. If installs arrive but users do not activate or pay, more posting alone does not solve the business.

## 20 This week and the standard for reaching the goal

**This week:** complete the outstanding real-device checks, schedule the first interviews through permitted channels, recruit the small adult beta, film the three core demonstrations, and prepare one spreadsheet covering sources, purchase cohorts, routines, MRR, and spending. Verify production billing and analytics setup against the existing checklists. Keep most of the cash unspent.

**The strongest practical evidence of reaching the goal** is $2,000+ correctly normalized gross paid MRR across repeated snapshots, reconciled against store transactions, with losses and subscriptions set to cancel visible. A more durable business also has ongoing routine use, a measured source of replacement customers, and positive contribution after its costs. An annual-heavy first-year number can establish the milestone before it establishes renewal durability.

The path with the lowest cash risk is therefore **prove the routine → prove payment by strangers → find a repeatable demonstration → repeat qualified acquisition → retain users → fund small tests from settled proceeds if desired**. All six stages are measurable. None substitutes for the next, and none turns an uncertain consumer app into guaranteed revenue.
