# Indie playbooks and the content engine (research, 2026-10-08)

Part of the "edge to $10K MRR" study. This file covers one angle: small consumer iOS
subscription apps that went from $0 to $10K+ MRR in 2025–2026, failures where big
views made little money, what changed on TikTok, Reels and Shorts in 2026, and a
week-by-week content plan for November 2026 → March 2027.

Already covered elsewhere, so not repeated here: Erly, Brainrot, Unrot, Focus Friend,
Opal, Cal AI and Puff Count's headline story ([10K_MRR_PLAN.md](../10K_MRR_PLAN.md) §3),
competitor playbooks ([COMPETITOR_MARKETING.md](../COMPETITOR_MARKETING.md) §3),
slideshows ([SLIDESHOW_MARKETING.md](../SLIDESHOW_MARKETING.md)) and AI UGC
([AI_UGC_RESEARCH.md](../AI_UGC_RESEARCH.md)). A few of those apps come up again
below, only for new numbers.

**Evidence labels.** **V** = official source, or several independent sources agree.
**S** = one secondary source, or the founder's own claim. **U** = unclear, conflicting
or unconfirmed. Almost every revenue figure for an indie app is the founder's own
claim, so most rows are **S**. Survivorship bias applies everywhere: founders talk when
the numbers are good.

**Research limit.** The session's shared web-search budget ran out after about 37 of
my searches. A few planned lookups did not happen: Reddit threads searched directly,
TikTok's own help pages on app-store links, and anything public about Rivaldle and
Soulsdoku. The gaps are flagged where they matter.

## 1. Short version

1. **Views are not the product. Purchases per 1K views are.** Across the cases with
   usable numbers, views turned into installs at anywhere from **0.002 to ~13 per 1K
   views**. That is a 5,000× spread. What decides it is the content, not the platform.
2. **Content that sells has four things:** a viewer who has the problem, a hook that
   names that problem, the app *visibly solving it* inside the video, and an obvious
   next step (say the name, pin a comment, add an anchor link). Content that only gets
   views is usually entertaining *about* the app (a toy, a meme, a game) without
   showing a problem being fixed.
3. **Why 1M views made ~$1K/month before:** the web games were free, played in the
   browser, aimed at people looking for entertainment, and had no paywall to buy
   through. Even with Locturne's funnel, **1M views ≈ $450–600 of net MRR**. $10K needs
   roughly **15–25M views a year**, and those views have to be the *right* views.
4. **2026 platforms:** TikTok's US business moved to a new joint venture on
   **22 Jan 2026** (V). Its algorithm is being retrained on US data. Average views per
   post fell **~31%** year on year while the number of posts rose **~72%** (S, Metricool).
   Instagram and YouTube both now cut reach for reposted and unoriginal content
   (Instagram from April 2026, Shorts from 1 Oct 2026, S). YouTube now counts a view
   from the first frame (24 Aug 2026), so Shorts view counts are inflated. Use
   **engaged views** to compare platforms.
5. **The plan in §6:** four weeks of hook testing in November, narrowing in December
   with a bank of videos filmed in advance, launch in January measured on installs and
   payers per 1K views, paid micro-creators only from revenue, and a scale-or-rethink
   decision at the end of March. Each stage has numeric kill and scale thresholds.

### 2.2 What each one teaches, in one line

- **Flame:** volume *with control*. Change one variable per batch and log it. The 150
  a day is the extreme. The spreadsheet is the part to copy.
- **Pushscroll:** a viral *concept* video is not distribution. They launched to six
  months of nothing. The breakthrough was a **repeatable format** plus a
  comment-to-DM funnel. They paid flat retainers to ~2,000 influencers they messaged
  and lost about $2K. They say the problem was the download rate, not the video.
- **GlowUp:** check demand in comments *before* building. Add the in-video link only
  after a video starts moving. They say adding it under ~1K views can kill the video.
  Most downloads came through the anchor link.
- **Stronger:** one format reposted 300 times can beat 300 new ideas. They chose a
  hard paywall and gave up some virality for revenue.
- **Coconote:** their 41M-view video converted badly (see §3.3). They aimed at the
  **buyer** (mums), not only the user. Their price went *up*.
- **CutCoach and 3AK:** tiny but **high-intent** audiences. A 300-view video aimed at
  exactly the right person beat a viral video aimed at nobody. The founder *was* the
  audience.
- **Snag:** let organic views pick the ad creative. Never write ads from scratch.
- **"We cracked TikTok":** one viral hit decays month by month. Without a repeatable
  format the curve goes down.

### 2.3 Patterns that hold across all of them

| Pattern | Seen in | Fit for Locturne |
|---|---|---|
| A **repeatable format**, reposted many times, beats one-off ideas | Stronger, Flame, Bloom, GlowUp, Pushscroll | High. The downstairs unlock is one fixed shot you can repeat forever. |
| **Several accounts**, each debuting a proven video | Flame (15–20), GlowUp (~7), Bloom (20+), Coconote (16+) | Medium. Two or three honest accounts, not farms ([AI_UGC_RESEARCH.md](../AI_UGC_RESEARCH.md) rules out fake personas). |
| The **product visibly working** inside the video | Pushscroll (push-ups on camera), Erly, GlowUp (makeup reveal), CutCoach | High. Apps asleep → walk downstairs → apps wake. |
| A **comment-driven CTA** ("comment 'app'", pinned reply) | Pushscroll (ManyChat), Erly, Bloom | High, and free. |
| **Hard paywall after a long, invested onboarding** | Stronger (+25%), Puff Count, Coconote, GlowUp | Already the plan. |
| **Premium price held or raised** | Coconote ($99.99 → $129) | Supports $59.99. |
| **Founder is part of the audience** | CutCoach, 3AK, Puff Count | High. You are a student who can't get out of bed on time. |
| **Months of nothing before it works** | Pushscroll (6 months), Bloom ("90 days"), HabitKit (2.5 years) | Plan for it. |

## 3. Views → revenue: what separates selling content from view-only content

### 3.1 The numbers that exist

Every row is a derived ratio from the founder's own claims, so treat each one as a
rough size.

| Case | Views | Installs | Installs per 1K views | Money | Why it converted (or didn't) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CutCoach niche posts | 200–500 per post | 10–15/day | **~20–100** (stated as "5–10% of views") | ~$30 each | Exact audience, exact problem (U: the ratio looks very high) |
| Pushscroll creator post | 38K | 200–500 | **5–13** | — | Calisthenics audience = right audience |
| Flame (8 months) | 58M | 250K | **4.3** | $10K MRR → **~$170 MRR per 1M views** | Volume of tested hooks |
| Puff Count first hit | 2.6M | 4,000 in 24h | **1.5** | — | A format copied from a proven video in the niche |
| GlowUp viral video | 48M | 60K | **1.25** | ~$12.5K that day → **~$260 per 1M views** | Demo with a reveal; anchor link |
| Playkit creator example | 20M+ | ~1,000 | **0.05** | — | **No call to action** |
| Reddit quit-smoking founder | 35K | 2 | **0.06** | — | Link in bio only |
| Reddit commenter | 180M | "a few hundred" | **~0.002** | — | Viewers liked the creator, not the app (U) |
| Coconote Minecraft video | 41M | "converted poorly" | — | — | Product shown as a **toy** |
| lofi.co reel (web, for comparison) | 1.8M | 12.5K sign-ups | 6.9 sign-ups | $5.6K | A paid influencer whose audience matched |

The planning figure used elsewhere, **1.5–2 installs per 1K views**
([10K_MRR_PLAN.md](../10K_MRR_PLAN.md) §2), sits in the middle of this range. It is
reasonable for broad organic content, but only if the video shows the mechanic and
has a call to action. Without those, expect 0.05.

### 3.2 What separates them

1. **Problem intent in the audience.** Calisthenics viewers for a push-up app, wrestlers
   for a weight-cut app, people who quit vaping for a vape counter. These convert at
   5–100 per 1K. General entertainment viewers convert at 0.05 or less.
2. **The hook names a pain the viewer has, in their words.** "I've hit snooze 9 times."
   "I lose an hour in bed on TikTok." It should not describe a feature.
3. **The app is the payoff, on screen, before the end.** Stronger's slideshow rule is
   "show the app by slide 2 or 3". Erly's is "alarm goes off → push-ups → alarm off."
   If the app isn't the climax, it's a meme.
4. **A visible next step.** Say the app's name aloud and on screen. Pin a comment.
   Use "comment 'sleep' and I'll DM it" (Pushscroll got ~33% click-through on the DM
   link). On TikTok, add an anchor link once the video has traction (GlowUp). The
   20M-view, 1,000-install example had no call to action.
5. **Comments that ask "what app?" beat raw views.** One agency claims a 200K-view
   video with 500 engaged comments outsells a 2.6M-view video with 200 passive ones
   (S, no data). Treat the number of comments asking for the app per 1K views as the
   **leading indicator**, because it shows up within hours. Sign-ups take days.
6. **The audience can pay.** US users convert at roughly double the European rate.
   Pushscroll saw US ARPU of ~$0.70 against ~$0.30 for under-18s outside the US (S).
   Teen-heavy viral audiences inflate installs, not revenue.
7. **The download has to lead to a sale.** At a hard paywall, RevenueCat's 2026
   benchmark is a **10.7% median Day-35 trial-to-paid rate, against 2.1% for
   freemium**, and **$3.09 revenue per install at day 60, against $0.38** (V,
   RevenueCat). Content brings people to the paywall. The paywall and onboarding sell.
8. **One format, repeated.** A single viral hit decays: $41K → $32K → $27K over three
   months. Formats compound (Stronger, Flame).

### 3.3 Why the founder's ~1M views made ~$1K/month, and how to avoid it

This is inference from the brief plus the patterns above. I could not research the
two games directly.

| Likely cause | What it looked like for Rivaldle/Soulsdoku | Locturne fix |
|---|---|---|
| **No purchase step** | Free web games played in the browser. The "conversion" was a play, not a payment. Money came from ads or tips at entertainment RPMs. | A hard paywall after an invested onboarding. Every video points to one paid app. |
| **Entertainment intent, not problem intent** | League and Soulsborne fans watched for fun. They had no problem to solve. This is the Coconote "toy" trap. | Hooks about a real pain: morning scrolling, snoozing, being late. The viewer has to recognise themselves. |
| **No recurring reason to pay** | A daily puzzle is a habit, but it isn't a problem the player would pay to fix. | Bedtime and morning blocking work every night (Opal credits scheduled blocks for retention). |
| **Volume was far too low for this kind of money** | ~1M views per project. | At Locturne's funnel, 1M views ≈ 1,500–2,000 installs ≈ 105–140 annual subs ≈ **$450–600 net MRR**. $10K needs ~15–25M views a year (~50–70K views a day), spread over repeatable formats. |
| **Spikes, not a format** | Probably a few big posts, then quiet (an assumption). | Pick a winner, then repost it many times on 2–3 accounts. Log every hook. |

**The big lesson:** last time, the views were real. What was missing was a **buy step**
and **buyer intent**. For Locturne, a video only counts if it makes someone think
"that's me, I need that" and then shows them where to get it.

## 4. The 2026 platforms

### 4.1 TikTok

| Change | Detail | Label |
|---|---|---|
| **New US owner** | TikTok USDS Joint Venture LLC took over the US business on **22 Jan 2026**. Oracle, Silver Lake and MGX hold 15% each, ByteDance 19.9%. CEO Adam Presser. Same app, no new download. | V |
| **Algorithm retrained** | The joint venture's mandate is to retrain, test and secure the recommendation system on US data in Oracle's cloud. ByteDance licenses the core algorithm. No public before/after benchmark exists. | V for the mandate, U for the effect |
| **Moderation moved, commerce didn't** | The joint venture has final say on moderation, which decides For You eligibility. Ads Manager, Shop and creator payouts stayed with ByteDance. | S |
| **Reach per post down** | Metricool, 2.31M posts: views per post **−31%**, reach −29%, interactions −31% (Jan–Feb 2026 against the same months of 2025). Videos posted **+72%**, image/carousel posts ~**+140%**. Mid-size and big accounts fell ~40%. | S (vendor sample) |
| | Social Insider: views −23% year on year. Engagement rate by views fell to **3.85%** in Q2 2026, from a steady ~4.2–4.3%. | S |
| | Claims of a "20–40% reach drop" after the joint venture took over. | U |
| **Video beats carousels** | Video gets ~5× the reach and ~6× the interactions of image/carousel posts. A post gets ~96% of its reach in its first 10 days. Best window 6–9pm, peak 8pm. | S (Metricool) |
| **Unoriginal-content flags** | Reposted or watermarked material can lose For You eligibility. Creator blogs say repeated flags suppress the whole account. | U (blogs only) |
| **Disclosure** | Promotional posts are expected to use the commercial-content toggle, or they risk losing For You placement. | S |
| **App-store links** | Since 2023, only business accounts can put App Store links in the bio. Status in 2026 is unconfirmed. | V for 2023, U now |
| **Search** | ~49% of US consumers say they use TikTok as a search engine (Adobe survey). Creator Search Insights shows real search demand. | S |

**What it means:** more posts are fighting over less attention per post. Volume alone
works less well than it did in 2024. Repeatable *original* video beats recycled
slideshows. Measure everything over a 10-day window.

### 4.2 Instagram Reels

| Change | Detail | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Originality rules extended | **30 Apr 2026**: the originality ranking rules for Reels now also cover photos and carousels. Reposts, watermarks or simple edits get less reach. Accounts recover if most of their posts over a rolling 30 days are original. | S |
| Ranking signals | Watch time, **sends (DM shares) per reach**, likes per reach. Sends count most for reaching non-followers. | S |
| **Trial Reels** | Shows a Reel only to non-followers first. You see results after 24h, and it can auto-share to followers if it does well within 72h. It is a free A/B test for hooks. | V (TechCrunch 2024), S for auto-share |
| Long-term picture | Bernstein: TikTok is better for a sudden hit, Instagram is better for turning followers into money. In 2023 Instagram passed TikTok in downloads. | S |

**What it means:** post every winning hook as a Trial Reel first. Optimise for **sends**:
"send this to the friend who snoozes 9 times". Always upload the clean original
file, never the TikTok export with its watermark.

### 4.3 YouTube Shorts

| Change | Detail | Label |
|---|---|---|
| View counting | From **24 Aug 2026**, a view counts from the first frame. The old definition is now called "engaged views". Public counts go up, and earnings are unchanged. | V (YouTube post via several outlets) |
| Originality | From **1 Oct 2026**, original Shorts rank higher and channels that mostly re-upload get less reach. Cutting Shorts from your own footage is probably fine. | S |
| Scale | ~200B Shorts views a day. Search and recommendations favour how-to content that stays useful for a long time. | S |

**What it means:** Shorts is the third platform, for evergreen "how I stopped scrolling
in bed" and setup clips. **Compare platforms on engaged views**, or the per-1K maths
will flatter Shorts.

### 4.4 What works now

- Your **own footage** of your **own product**, posted natively on each platform
  without watermarks. All three platforms now reward this and penalise reposts.
- **Person-led or character-led** content over faceless templates. This matches
  [10K_MRR_PLAN.md](../10K_MRR_PLAN.md) §3, where the faceless Reveille made $121/month.
- **One proven format, many variations.** Change the hook text, location and time of
  day, and keep the mechanic shot the same.
- **Comment and DM funnels** instead of bio links.
- **Organic winners become ads** (Snag, Opal), with spend funded only from revenue.

## 5. Measuring: the funnel to track per post

| Stage | Metric | Planning number | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attention | Views (engaged views on Shorts), 3-second hold, completion | — | Platform analytics |
| Intent | **Comments asking "what app?" per 1K views** | Watch the trend. Aim for ≥0.5 (opinion). | Count by hand |
| Pre-launch | **Waitlist sign-ups per 1K views** | ≥3 = a working hook; <1 = change it | UTM per account and format |
| Install | **Installs per 1K views** | 1.5–2 (plan); ≥3 strong; <0.5 means the content and product don't match | App Store Connect sources, "where did you hear about us?", spike matching |
| Pay | **Install → paid** at the hard paywall | 7% (plan); RevenueCat median 10.7% trial-to-paid | RevenueCat |
| Money | **Net MRR per 1M views** | ~$450–600 at plan rates | Derived |

Spike matching is what Erly does: line up view spikes against conversion spikes and
accept loose attribution. Most installs land in the "organic/unattributed" bucket
(~75% in Adapty's 2025 data, S).

## 6. Content operating plan: November 2026 → March 2027

**Assumptions:** 15–20 hours a week alongside classes and app work. $0 before launch.
After launch, creator spend comes only from revenue, capped at 50% of the previous
month's net. Launch is early January. Two honest accounts (founder and Loc), with a
third added only as a debut account for proven videos. No faceless farms and no fake
personas. This builds on the dated plans in
[COMPETITOR_MARKETING.md](../COMPETITOR_MARKETING.md) §5 and
[SLIDESHOW_MARKETING.md](../SLIDESHOW_MARKETING.md) §7 and doesn't replace them.

### 6.1 Weekly time budget

| Block | Hours/week | What |
|---|---|---|
| Film | 4–5 | Batch-film 2 sessions a week: the downstairs unlock, morning struggle, talking to camera |
| Edit and post | 5–6 | 14–20 native posts a week across TikTok, Reels and Shorts |
| Engage | 3–4 | 15 min before each posting session; 10 min/day of Loc comments on sleep and doomscrolling videos; reply to every "what app" comment |
| Measure | 1–2 | Sunday: update the hook spreadsheet and decide the coming week's 3 tests |
| Research | 1 | Creator Search Insights; save the 10 top videos in the niche each week |

### 6.2 Week by week

**Prep (12–31 Oct, ~5 hrs/week, alongside building)**
- Puff Count-style study: search "can't wake up", "stop scrolling in bed", "bed
  rotting", "phone addiction", "snooze" on TikTok. Sort by most liked. Save 50 videos
  into a sheet with columns for hook, story, CTA and views.
- Reserve handles. Warm up the accounts. Set up UTM waitlist links per account and
  format, plus the "where did you hear about us?" onboarding question.
- Build the spreadsheet: post ID, account, platform, format, hook text, views (and
  engaged views on Shorts), "what app" comments, sign-ups, and later installs.

**Stage 1: Hook lab (weeks 1–4, 2–29 Nov)**

| Week | Output | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (2–8 Nov) | 2 accounts × 1 post/day, cross-posted natively = ~14 TikTok + 14 Reels (as Trial Reels) + Shorts | Five hook families: snooze pain, scroll-in-bed pain, "my phone knows if I went downstairs", Loc's sass, the student-founder story |
| 2 (9–15 Nov) | Same volume | First **real footage** of the downstairs unlock as soon as a build allows |
| 3 (16–22 Nov) | Same, plus 3 remakes of the best post so far | One variable per remake (Flame's rule) |
| 4 (23–29 Nov) | Same | Thanksgiving week: film and bank 10+ videos |

*Metrics:* waitlist sign-ups per 1K views by hook family, and "what app?" comments per 1K.
*Kill:* a hook family with 10+ posts, median views under 300, nothing over 5K, and
fewer than 1 sign-up per 1K. Drop it.
*Scale:* any post over 50K views with ≥3 sign-ups per 1K. Make 5–10 remakes the next
week, and debut the best on a fresh third account.
*Stage gate (29 Nov):* at least one format at ≥3 sign-ups per 1K and ≥300 waitlist
total. If not, spend December's first week on new hooks, not more volume.

**Stage 2: Narrow and bank (weeks 5–8, 30 Nov–27 Dec)**

| Week | Output | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 5 (30 Nov–6 Dec) | 2/day on the best account, 1/day on the other | 2–3 winning formats only |
| 6–7 (7–20 Dec) | **Finals.** Post from the bank at ~1/day per account | Engagement only; no new filming |
| 8 (21–27 Dec) | Winter break: film **30–40 videos** for January | New Year hooks: "my phone stays asleep till I'm up" |

*Metrics:* the same, plus follower growth on the winning account.
*Kill:* any account with a 7-day median under 500 views after 30+ posts. Fold its
time into the better account.
*Scale:* if one format keeps ≥3 sign-ups per 1K for 2 weeks, it becomes the
"house format" and gets ≥50% of posts.
*Stage gate (27 Dec):* waitlist ≥1,000 is on track. Under 300 means the launch still
happens, but plan January as more testing, not scaling.

**Stage 3: Launch (weeks 9–12, 28 Dec–24 Jan)**

| Week | Output | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 9 (28 Dec–3 Jan) | Launch content, 2–3/day across accounts | "New year, my apps sleep till I walk downstairs"; email the waitlist; Apple featuring and press per COMPETITOR_MARKETING |
| 10 (4–10 Jan) | 2/day | Switch every CTA to "it's called Locturne", plus a pinned comment and DM keyword |
| 11 (11–17 Jan) | 2/day from the bank | Classes start, so time drops; post from the bank |
| 12 (18–24 Jan) | 2/day | First **2–3 paid creator tests** ($50–150 each, $2–3 CPM guaranteed) as soon as revenue exists |

*Metrics now:* **installs per 1K views** and **install → paid**.
*Diagnosis rules:*
- Plenty of views but under 0.5 installs per 1K means the content and product don't
  match. Fix the hook and the visible mechanic, not the volume.
- Installs per 1K at or above 1.5 but under 4% paid means the onboarding or paywall
  is the problem, not the content. Stop adding content hours and fix onboarding.
- Paid at or above 7% means the funnel works. Volume is now the job.

*Stage gate (24 Jan):* in the first 3 weeks, ≥1,000 installs and ≥5% paid.

**Stage 4: Scale what works (weeks 13–17, 25 Jan–28 Feb)**

- Keep 2/day organic on the winning account, 1/day on the other.
- Creators: expand to 5–10, but only those whose installs per 1K beat the organic
  average after 2 videos. Give each the winning template (Erly, Snag). Budget at most
  50% of January's net revenue.
- Put a small Spark or creator-licence ad budget behind any organic post that beats
  2× the median installs per 1K. Cap it at revenue-funded spend.
- Valentine's / "sleep together" and exam-season hooks for variety, keeping the house
  format.

*Kill:* any creator below the organic installs per 1K after 2 videos. Any ad with
cost per payer above 50% of first-year net (~$25).
*Scale:* a creator who beats organic gets a monthly retainer (Cal AI style).

**Stage 5: Decision month (weeks 18–21, 1–31 Mar)**

- Spring break: a second filming bank of 30+ videos.
- Read the numbers against the MRR model ([03_mrr_model.csv](03_mrr_model.csv)): base
  case month 3 ≈ 1,800 installs/month and ≈ $1,300 net MRR.

| End-of-March result | Call |
|---|---|
| ≥ $2K net MRR and installs per 1K ≥ 1.5 | **Scale:** pay a student editor or creator part-time from revenue; add a third account; double the creator budget |
| $700–2K net MRR | **Stay the course:** content works but there isn't enough of it. Raise output and creators within the cash cap. |
| $300–700 net MRR | **Fix one link:** find the weakest stage in §5 and spend April only on it |
| < $300 net MRR **and** < 0.5 installs per 1K across 3+ formats | **Rethink the hook-product match:** the audience isn't feeling the pain on screen. Go back to the November research, or reconsider positioning ([10K_MRR_PLAN.md](../10K_MRR_PLAN.md) §8 risks). |

### 6.3 Rules that apply all the way through

1. Every video shows the **mechanic** or the **pain**, and names the app (after launch).
2. Upload natively to each platform. No watermarks and no reposting other people's
   clips.
3. Add the TikTok anchor link or pinned CTA only after a post shows traction (~1K+
   views).
4. Change one variable per remake and log it.
5. Judge posts after **10 days**, not 24 hours.
6. Grade content by **sign-ups, installs or payers per 1K views**. Never by views
   alone.

## 7. Open questions for the founder

1. What did Rivaldle and Soulsdoku actually monetise with (ads, tips, nothing)? Your
   real numbers would replace the inference in §3.3.
2. Are you comfortable filming your face daily? The plan assumes yes. CutCoach, 3AK and
   Puff Count all leaned on it.
3. Is a roommate or friend available two mornings a week to film the downstairs walk?
