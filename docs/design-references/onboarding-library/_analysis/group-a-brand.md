# Group A — brand- and personality-led onboarding (2026-10-05)

Apps: Duolingo, Finch, Headspace, Calm Sleep, CARROT Weather, Focus Friend, How We Feel.
Sources: each app's `FLOW.md` (row numbers) and its free images (`001–008.jpg`, `showcase-N.jpg`).
Compared against the current Locturne flow in `_locturne-before/grid0–6.jpg`.

## The one-line diagnosis

None of the onboarding screens in these apps sounds like a survey. In the best ones, every
question screen has the mascot on it reacting to the answer the user just picked.
Locturne's quiz screens (nights → time-back) are a sans-serif question over five black pills,
with a large empty band in the middle. Loc only speaks on the separate screens around them
(deal, tried-echo, math). So the quiz feels like a form that Loc wraps, not a talk with Loc.

## 1. Per app

### Duolingo — $53M/mo
- **Screens to paywall:** 37. The paywall is row 38 (05:51), after a full first lesson and account creation.
- **Arc:** Duo introduces himself → 7 questions → a real 2-minute lesson → "Learning legend!" → streak commitment → profile → paywall.
- **Personalization:**
  - The owl asks every question in a speech bubble (008, showcase-2/3).
  - The bubble *replies to the topic*: "Best thing to pack is the local language!" sits above the motivation list with "Prepare for travel" picked (showcase-2).
  - Each option carries a tone label ("5 min/day — Casual … 20 min/day — Intense", showcase-3).
  - Projects the user's goal forward: "learn 25 words in the first week" (row 13).
  - A course-building loader with the user's language in it (006).
  - A result card with the user's own stats: Total XP 25 / 100% / 1:53 (showcase-5).
- **Brand:**
  - Duo has many poses: waving (003), dizzy-eyed for "Just 7 quick questions" (004), reading on the loader (006), holding a pencil (showcase-4).
  - The CTA "I'M COMMITTED" is the user's own words (showcase-3).
  - "10 in a row" celebration overlays (row 26).
- **Best screens:** showcase-2 (bubble reacts to the answer topic), showcase-3 (tone-labelled options + "I'm committed"), showcase-5 (personal result card).
- **Apple risk:** none in this part. "Don't lose your progress!" (showcase-6) is loss framing but allowed.

### Finch — $1M/mo
- **Screens to paywall:** 31. The paywall is row 32 (02:37).
- **Arc:** hatch an egg → name, gender and trait for the pet → the pet asks *your* name → a ~12-question wellbeing survey → "Generating your self-care goals with Piper…" → starter plan → paywall.
- **Personalization:**
  - The user makes the mascot: chooses an egg (003), its pronouns (006), its name with a Shuffle button (007) and its trait (008, "Piper cares about… Compassion").
  - The pet then asks for the user's name (row 9) and uses it.
  - The answers build a visible plan (rows 30–31, showcase-4).
  - The plan contains "Get out of bed" as goal #1 (showcase-6).
- **Brand:**
  - The hatch moment (004 → 005, "You hatched a birb!").
  - The pet reacts with a stat gain after a philosophical question (row 11: "Logic +").
  - Piper's energy bar has to fill before her adventure (row 12, showcase-7).
- **Best screens:** 003→005 (the hatch), 008 (choosing who the mascot is), row 30–31 / showcase-4 (the plan built with the mascot).
- **Apple risk:** "Biggest deal ever! One-time 43% OFF when you start your free trial now … See one-time FREE offer" (rows 33–34, showcase-5). This is a one-time-discount pressure screen; it is close to the fake-urgency pattern that review now flags.

### Headspace — $2M/mo
- **Screens to paywall:** 11. The paywall is row 12 (01:06).
- **Arc:** the first screen is an *experience*, not a question: "Breathe in / Breathe out" with the orange face rising and sinking (002–003). Then account creation → 3 value cards → 1 goal question → trial-timeline paywall → three guided breaths over a blue sky (rows 18–34) → "How did that make you feel?" (row 35).
- **Personalization:** weak. One multi-select goal question (row 10), plus "Good Afternoon, Julia" on home (showcase-6).
- **Brand:**
  - The face *is* the breathing animation. The signature motion carries the brand before any copy does.
  - A three-step trial timeline (showcase-4: Today / In 12 days / In 14 days).
  - A smiling bell illustration on the notification ask (row 16).
- **Best screens:** 002/003 (signature motion as the welcome), showcase-4 (trial timeline), row 35 (asks how the taste felt).
- **Apple risk:** none. But the account wall before any value (004–006) costs conversion. Locturne should not copy it.

### Calm Sleep — <$5k/mo (Calm's sleep spin-off, not main Calm)
- **Screens to paywall:** 20. The paywall is row 21 (01:49).
- **Arc:** night landscape with "Leave the day behind" (002) → content carousels → "Let's start by building your perfect sleep plan" (006) → six questions → "We're crafting your sleep plan" checklist (row 13) → sleep schedule dial (showcase-3) → notifications, ATT and sign-up → "Your plan is ready. Unlock it now." → paywall.
- **Personalization:**
  - Pre-announces the six areas it will ask about (006). A "1/6" counter.
  - A circular bedtime/wake dial with a moon handle and a sun handle shows both ends of the night on one control (showcase-3).
- **Brand:** night-blue gradient and white pill buttons, very close to Locturne. It has *no voice*: every line is generic wellness copy. That is the cautionary tale: the same look as Locturne, but with no character, and it earns <$5k.
- **Best screens:** showcase-3 (bedtime and wake as one picture), 006 (sets expectations for the questions).
- **Apple risk:**
  - **App rating prompt shown during first use** (rows 43–45: "Are you enjoying the app?", then stars, then "Write a Review"). Per the brief this is now rejected.
  - ATT (row 16) is asked before any value.
  - The "We're crafting your sleep plan / Analyzing your sleep patterns" checklist (showcase-2) claims analysis the app doesn't do.

### CARROT Weather — $250k/mo
- **Screens to paywall:** 5. The paywall is row 6 (00:35).
- **Arc:** logo → **"Personality — Choose a personality for your weather assistant"** with a slider → "Greetings, meatbag. I, CARROT, am your new weather robot." → first-person paywall → settings.
- **Personalization:**
  - The user chooses *how the mascot talks to them*: a slider from a briefcase icon to a bomb icon. The tooltip changes live ("Snarky — CARROT will get a teensy bit mean").
  - Two joke toggles below it: "Speech: On / Politics: Off" (002).
- **Brand:**
  - Pure voice; there is almost no art, only the logo circle (004/005). This is the closest match to Locturne's "voice is the mascot" constraint.
  - A joke in the fine print: "any extreme weather you experience after downloading me is strictly coincidental" (005).
  - The paywall is in first person: "Try my very best features. First week's on me." (006).
  - Tapping the logo answers back: "You just poked my ocular sensor. Don't do that." (showcase-7).
  - The home screen keeps the voice ("My army of robots have set up this garden…", showcase-5).
- **Best screens:** 002 (personality slider), 005 (voice-only intro with a fine-print joke), showcase-7 (poke easter egg).
- **Apple risk:** none.

### Focus Friend (Hank Green) — $150k/mo
- **Screens to paywall:** 15. A soft Pro screen is row 16 (01:28). The hard paywall comes later, from the shop (row 37).
- **Arc:**
  - Meet the bean → name him ("Uhh… who am I? (You can change this later. I'm still figuring out who I am)", 004/005) → "Bean?… I like it!" (006).
  - Backstory: "A fragment of bean fell into your phone. Now I live here." (008).
  - The problem, told as his problem: "Whenever you pick up your phone, I get distracted and drop my stitches" (showcase-3).
  - The user replies "I want to help" (row 12) → "The longer you focus, the more knitting I can finish" (showcase-4) → "Let's focus together".
- **Personalization:** the name only. But every CTA is the user's line in a conversation: "HELLO!", "YOUR NAME IS…", "HOW DID YOU GET HERE?", "A BEAN?" (003, 004, 006, 007).
- **Brand:**
  - The buttons as dialogue is the whole trick. You read the onboarding as a chat, not a funnel.
  - The mascot's state is tied to the user's phone use.
  - A hand-drawn font and paper buttons.
- **Best screens:** 004/005 (naming with a self-aware aside), 007/008 (answer buttons that move the story), showcase-3 (the problem told as the mascot's problem).
- **Apple risk:**
  - The system notification prompt appears on the very first frame, with no context (001). That is poor practice.
  - Guilt on quitting: "Are you sure? Bean's knitting will unravel. They'll be really sad." (showcase-6). This is not a rejection risk, but it breaks Locturne's no-punishment rule.

### How We Feel — <$5k/mo (free, donation-funded; 006)
- **Screens to paywall:** none. The onboarding ends around row 19.
- **Arc:**
  - Logo → "A Journal for Your Wellbeing" → value cards (word cloud, expert video, emotion list) → "It's free".
  - "Terms & Privacy — Your data is yours" (007) → "Hi and welcome" with **Skip setup** (008).
  - "Before jumping in, let's explore why you're here" 2/8 (showcase-2) → "Let's do your first check-in" with a 2×2 energy/pleasantness grid (showcase-3) → zoom into the colour quadrant and pick a word (showcase-4).
- **Personalization:**
  - The first real action *is* the product (a check-in), not a question about the product.
  - The selected word is defined live at the bottom ("Motivated — enthusiastic about doing something", showcase-4).
- **Brand:**
  - Big serif headlines on black with white pill buttons. This is the closest *visual* peer to Locturne, and evidence the look reads premium.
  - Its signature is one unique input: the colour mood meter.
- **Best screens:** showcase-3/4 (a unique input that gives an instant definition), 007 (a plain-English privacy promise), 008 (Skip setup).
- **Apple risk:** none.

## 2. Steal list for Locturne (ranked)

Rules held throughout:
- Monochrome. No glow.
- The moon itself never changes.
- No mascot art.
- Big italic serif only when Loc is speaking.
- Native SwiftUI controls only (Slider, Picker, sheets via @expo/ui).
- Loc's jokes land on Loc, the apps or the phone, never on the user's character.

### 1. Loc reacts live on every quiz screen — steps `nights`, `night-minutes`, `morning-minutes`, `found`, `tried`, `time-back`
- **What it shows:**
  - Use the empty band between the question and the pills (`grid0` #3–4, `grid1`, `grid2` #1, #3).
  - When the user taps a pill, a single italic serif line from Loc fades in there, before Continue. Change it if they re-pick.
  - Keep the question in sans (that's the form). Loc's line is the reply.
- **Lines:**
  - `nights`:
    - "One more video. Then twelve more." → *"Twelve. Rookie numbers. I do forty."*
    - "I can't sleep, so I scroll." → *"Scrolling isn't sleeping. I checked."*
    - "I lose track of time." → *"Time's fine. It's right where you left it."*
    - "Honestly, all of it." → *"Honest. I like that. Bit worrying."*
  - `night-minutes`:
    - Under 10 → *"Ten minutes? Why are you here. Not complaining."*
    - 10–30 → *"Respectable. Mostly."*
    - 30–60 → *"An episode. Every night."*
    - 1–2 h → *"That's a film. Nightly."*
    - 2+ h → *"Raccoon hours. I know them well."*
  - `morning-minutes`:
    - 15–30 → *"The snooze-and-scroll. A classic."*
    - 1+ hour → *"Your phone gets up before you do."*
  - `found`:
    - TikTok → *"TikTok sent you here to quit TikTok. Poetic."*
    - A friend → *"Thank them. Or don't. I'm not your mum."*
  - `tried`:
    - Willpower → *"Willpower's lovely. It goes to bed before you do."*
    - Phone in another room → *"And then you went and got it."*
    - Nothing yet → *"Good. Nothing to undo."*
  - `time-back`:
    - Read → *"Paper. Doesn't buzz."*
    - Work out → *"Ugh. Fine."*
    - Sleep more → *"Bold. I support it."*
- **Why:** this is the Duolingo mechanic (showcase-2: the bubble answers the topic picked). It turns six identical survey screens into six exchanges with a character, and it proves the app heard the answer. It is the biggest single fix for "they all feel the same", for copy cost only.

### 2. New step `voice` — "How grumpy should I be?" (after `deal`, before `nights`)
- **What it shows:**
  - A native SwiftUI `Slider` with 3 detents.
  - Above it, a sans heading: "How grumpy should I be?"
  - Below it, Loc's italic line changes live with the detent:
    - **Mild** → *"I'll be nice. It'll be weird for both of us."*
    - **Grumpy** (default) → *"The usual. Mornings are a crime."*
    - **Unbearable** → *"You asked for this. Remember that at 7 a.m."*
  - Optional joke footer in small sans, CARROT-style: "Swearing: Off. Opinions: On."
- **What it drives:** the bedtime and morning notification copy, the `first-morning` screen and the "No. Tap Fine." line. Write 3 variants of ~10 lines, not the whole app.
- **Why:**
  - This is CARROT 002, the best brand-and-personalization screen in the group, from a voice-only mascot (exactly Locturne's v1 limit).
  - The user is now *configuring Loc*, which Finch (008, choose a trait) and CARROT both show makes people own the mascot.
  - It also gives everyone else's choice a payoff later in the flow.

### 3. Answer buttons become the user's lines — every Continue button
- **What it shows:** replace generic "Continue" with the user's reply to whatever Loc just said. `deal` already does this ("Ask away").
  - `tried-echo` "Continue" → **"Fair."**
  - `math` → **"Go on, then."**
  - `reveal` "Let's fix this" (keep)
  - `screen-time` → **"Fine. Apple."**
  - `walk` "Start walking" → **"Ugh. Walking."**
  - `armed` → **"Goodnight, Loc."**
  - `tomorrow` "Try it" (keep)
- **Why:** this is Focus Friend's whole trick (003 "HELLO!", 006 "HOW DID YOU GET HERE?", 007 "A BEAN?", row 12 "I WANT TO HELP") and Duolingo's "I'M COMMITTED" (showcase-3). It turns the funnel into a conversation, for zero design cost.

### 4. `math` shows Loc doing *their* arithmetic, line by line
- **What it shows:** today the screen lists generic bullets ("Nights in bed with your phone…", `grid2` #4). Replace them with the user's own numbers, appearing one line at a time in sans tabular figures:
  - "45 min a night × 6 nights = 4½ h"
  - "20 min a morning × 6 mornings = 2 h"
  - "Rounded down, because I'm generous = 6 h"
- Loc's italic line underneath stays: *"You said twenty minutes. I said nothing."*
- **Why:**
  - Finch (rows 30–31), Calm (row 13) and Duolingo (006) all show a plan or result being "built". Theirs are fake checklists ("Analyzing your sleep patterns").
  - Locturne can do it *honestly*, with real sums from the user's own answers. It is more personal and doesn't make claims Apple or users would distrust.
  - It makes the `reveal` number feel earned rather than asserted.

### 5. `nights-per-week` gets a signature input: seven small moons
- **What it shows:**
  - Replace the M–S circle chips (`grid1` #1) with seven small, flat monochrome moon glyphs labelled M–S (a native-feeling toggle row).
  - Tapping a night makes its moon go from full to dim, the same as the current chip toggle.
  - Header count: "6 nights a week".
  - Loc line: *"Sunday off? Respect."* when Sunday is unpicked; *"Every night. Okay. We'll talk."* when all 7 are picked.
- **Constraint:** the hero moon is not touched. These are small icons.
- **Why:** How We Feel's brand is one unique input (showcase-3/4, the mood meter). Calm's sleep dial (showcase-3) is the only memorable Calm screen. Locturne's moon is its loved asset, so a moon-shaped input brands a screen that is currently generic.

### 6. `wake` closes the loop with `bedtime`: a live "hours in bed" line
- **What it shows:** keep the native wheel picker. Under it, a live line that updates as the wheel turns: "11:30 PM → 7:00 AM · **7½ hours**". Loc adds a line by band:
  - < 7 h → *"Seven and a half. I'd take nine. I'm a raccoon."*
  - ≥ 8 h → *"Eight hours. Look at you."*
- **Why:**
  - Calm's dial (showcase-3) shows both ends of the night as one picture.
  - Locturne splits them across two screens and never joins them. Joining them makes the second answer clearly use the first, and that is what "personalized" feels like.

### 7. `walk` ends with a personal result card in Loc's voice
- **What it shows:** after the 20-step taste, a small card with three stats from the user's actual walk:
  - "20 steps"
  - "0:14"
  - "Loc's verdict: *suspiciously fast*" (or *"Leisurely. I respect it."* if it took over 40 s)
- Button: **"Again tomorrow. Unfortunately."**
- **Why:** Duolingo's first-lesson result (showcase-5: XP / accuracy / time) is the moment the product becomes *yours*. Headspace asks how the breaths felt (row 35). Locturne's taste currently just ends.

### 8. Callbacks to earlier answers at the moments that matter — `offer`, `armed`, `first-morning`
- **What it shows:** reuse `time-back`, `tried` and `nights`.
  - `offer` already does *"Slow mornings. My favorite kind."* (`grid5` #2). Keep it.
  - **`armed`:** *"You said 'Phone in another room' worked for a bit. This is the room. It's locked."*
  - **`first-morning`:** *"Downstairs. Good. Go have the slow morning you mentioned."*
- **Why:** Duolingo projects the goal forward (row 13) and Finch's plan echoes the survey (row 31). People feel heard when an answer from 15 screens ago comes back.

### 9. Poke Loc — hidden reaction on the `hello` moon
- **What it shows:** tapping the moon on `hello` (`grid0` #1) plays a light haptic and swaps the italic subtitle for one line, which returns after 3 s:
  - *"That's the moon. Leave it."*
  - A second tap: *"I'm trying to sleep."*
- **Constraint:** the moon does not move, change or glow. Only the text changes.
- **Why:** CARROT's "You just poked my ocular sensor. Don't do that." (showcase-7) is the kind of detail people screen-record. It costs one line and suits the user's TikTok marketing plan.

### 10. Optional nickname — `found` or new step `name`, with a skip
- **What it shows:**
  - A sans heading "What do I call you?", with a native text field and the placeholder "you".
  - Loc's line: *"Optional. It stays on this phone. I'm not writing it down, I can't hold a pen."*
  - Skip option: **"'You' is fine."**
  - The name is used only on `commit`, `armed` and morning notifications ("Morning, Sam. No.").
- **Constraint:** `deal` currently promises "No name". Change it to "No email, no account" if this ships.
- **Why:** Finch (row 9), Headspace (showcase-6) and Focus Friend (004) all use the name, and Finch's pet asking for it is warmer than a form. Ranked last because it touches a privacy promise that is itself on-brand.

## 3. Don't copy

- **Account or sign-up walls before value.** Headspace 004–006 and Calm rows 17–19. Locturne's no-account promise (`deal`) is a strength. How We Feel shows a privacy-first line ("Your data is yours", 007) reads premium.
- **Fake "building your plan" checklists.** Calm showcase-2 ("Analyzing your sleep patterns…") and Finch row 30. Use real arithmetic instead (steal #4).
- **One-time discount pressure.** Finch showcase-5 ("Biggest deal ever! One-time 43% OFF… See one-time FREE offer") and the paywall chain in rows 32–34. This is fake-urgency territory, and it contradicts the calm brand.
- **Rating prompts in onboarding or the first session.** Calm rows 43–45. Rejected now.
- **Mascot guilt when quitting.** Focus Friend showcase-6 ("Bean's knitting will unravel. They'll be really sad."). The emergency unlock and passes must stay guilt-free. Loc can grumble at himself, never make the user feel cruel.
- **Long health surveys.** Finch rows 17–29: sleep, stress, support network, mental-health conditions. Each one adds a screen that looks the same, and mental-health questions don't fit a bedtime-blocking app. Locturne's current question count is already at the limit. Make the questions *feel* different (steals #1, #5, #6) rather than adding more.
- **Mascot art, or brand colour as an accent.** Duolingo's owl poses and Finch's hatch need illustration, which v1 has none of. CARROT proves voice alone works. Don't reach for raccoon art to fix "feels the same".
- **A notification prompt with no context on the very first frame.** Focus Friend 001. Locturne's `armed` screen, which explains each permission before iOS asks, is better.
- **Generic wellness copy over a night-blue look.** Calm Sleep looks almost identical to Locturne and earns <$5k/mo. The look alone isn't the brand; Loc's voice is.
- **Widget, contacts and social detours during onboarding.** Duolingo rows 16–18 and 53–56. They are fine for a free app with growth loops, but noise before a hard paywall.
