## 2. Steal list for Locturne (ranked)

The pattern behind items 1–4: **the winners ask for the time first and then talk in it.**
Alarmy's first question is the alarm time (row 5). Sleep Cycle's first screen is
the alarm picker (`002.jpg`). Loóna asks bedtime at question 4 (`007.jpg`).
Locturne asks bedtime and wake at steps 14–15, so the 13 screens before that
can't say "11:30" or "7:00", and they read as a generic quiz.

### 1. Move `bedtime` and `wake` up to right after `deal`
- **What:** the new order starts hello → deal → **bedtime → wake** → nights → … Keep the current pickers and presets (grid3) exactly.
- **Loc line on `bedtime`:** *"When do your apps go to sleep? Pick a time. I'll be there."*
- **Why:** every later screen can now speak in the user's own clock. It is the cheapest change with the biggest effect on "these all feel the same." Evidence: Alarmy row 5, Sleep Cycle `002.jpg`, Loóna `007.jpg`.
- **Brand check:** it reuses existing native pickers, so it adds no new UI.

### 2. Rewrite the minute questions as clock-time questions in their times
- **`nights`:** "It's 11:30. You're in bed. Then what?" (same four answers).
- **`night-minutes`:** "Lights out at 11:30. When does the phone actually go down?" Answers are **clock times**, not durations: `11:40` · `Midnight` · `12:30` · `1 AM` · `Later. Don't ask.` Underneath, a thin horizontal strip from 11:30 extends to the chosen time as you tap.
- **`morning-minutes`:** "Alarm at 7:00. When do your feet hit the floor?" Answers: `7:05` · `7:15` · `7:30` · `8-ish`.
- **Why:** the user now sees their *own night* on each question, and the answer type changes from duration pills to times on a strip, which breaks the sameness. Wayk does this with "what time do you get out of bed" → "ideal wake time" → a goal screen with the time gained (rows 20–22). The math in `reveal` stays identical: you just subtract the times.

### 3. Replace `math` with "Your night, drawn" (a personal two-column timeline)
- **What:** a full screen in the style of Wayk `showcase-3.jpg` (row 15), but in Nocturne style: monochrome, thin white lines, serif times. Left column **"Your usual"**, right column **"With me"**, both built only from their answers:
  - **Your usual:** 11:30 · In bed. → 12:15 · Still on TikTok. → 7:00 · Alarm. → 7:01 · Instagram. → 7:20 · Feet on floor.
  - **With me:** 11:30 · TikTok, Instagram, YouTube go to sleep. → 7:00 · Alarm. They're still asleep. → 7:03 · Bottom of the stairs. Everyone's up.
  - **Footer:** "65 minutes back. Tonight." Loc, in italic serif, closes with: *"Same night. Less TikTok. I'd call that a win if I were awake."*
- **Why:** this is the screen that makes it obviously *their* app: the picture is built from their bedtime, their alarm, their scrolling time and (after `apps`, if you keep the order) their app names. Evidence: Wayk `showcase-3.jpg` and rows 45–46, RISE row 40 "SEE THE FUTURE" timeline. Today's `math` screen (grid2, the last panel) is a bullet list that carries none of this.
- **Brand check:** use no colour accents. "With me" gets the white line and "Your usual" a dimmer grey one. Do not use green "gained" badges or any glow.

### 4. Give every quiz answer an instant Loc reaction
- **What:** when the user taps a pill, one italic serif line fades in under the pills for about 0.8 s before the screen advances (the same treatment as "I hate stairs. That's the point." on `method`). One line per answer, written for that answer. Examples:
  - `nights` → "I can't sleep, so I scroll." → *"Scrolling is famously sleepy. Oh wait."*
  - `night-minutes` → "1 AM" → *"Ninety minutes. I've had shorter naps."*
  - `found` → TikTok → *"Ironic. Welcome anyway."*
  - `tried` → "Willpower" → *"How's that going. Don't answer."*
- **Why:** the brand rule is that big italic serif means Loc is talking. Right now Loc is silent on most quiz screens, so they read as a form. RISE expands the chosen option with a reaction (`showcase-2.jpg`), and ShutEye keeps a character mark on every question (`showcase-1.jpg`). A per-answer line costs nothing visually and is pure voice-as-mascot.
- **Note:** keep reduce-motion respected and let a second tap skip ahead.

### 5. Turn `deal` into a three-beat night demo (Sleep Cycle opener)
- **What:** the same three sections `deal` already has, but each one shows a small phone card on the haze, the way Sleep Cycle `002→004.jpg` plays the product before asking anything:
  1. **BEDTIME:** a lock-screen card reading 11:30 PM, where three app icons dim to 40% and get a small "asleep" label. Loc: *"Your apps go to sleep. So do I."*
  2. **MORNING:** a card at 7:00 AM where tapping TikTok shows the shield: "No." Loc: *"They stay asleep until you're up. Same as me."*
  3. **UP MEANS UP:** the `0.0 / 2.5 m down` meter from `tomorrow` (grid4) fills, and the icons undim. Loc: *"Downstairs. They wake up. I do too, unfortunately."*
- **Why:** Sleep Cycle, the top earner here, shows the night-to-morning loop in three frames before the welcome. Locturne's `deal` is currently three paragraphs of text that describe exactly those frames.
- **Brand check:** keep the moon and its transitions as they are. The cards sit on the existing haze, with no particles and no glow.

### 6. Let the haze tell the time of day
- **What:** night-question screens keep the night-blue haze. When the story moves to morning (`morning-minutes`, `tomorrow`, `walk`), the haze cools to a pale pre-dawn grey-blue with the same blur and no sun. At `armed` it returns to night ("Armed. Today at 11:30 PM.").
- **Why:** Wayk `002→003.jpg` (star night to morning), Sleep Cycle's single continuous sky, and RISE's continuous line all show that one backdrop changing with the story makes 20+ screens feel like one journey rather than 20 forms.
- **Hard limit:** **do not move, tint or animate the moon itself** (moon untouched rule). Change only the haze colour temperature, crossfaded inside the existing transitions. No sun graphic, no bloom.

### 7. A "wake receipt" preview at the end of `tomorrow` (and for real at `first-morning`)
- **What:** a small monochrome card, styled like a ticket stub:
  > LOCTURNE · TUE OCT 6
  > Apps asleep 11:30 PM – 7:03 AM
  > Woken by: one trip downstairs
  > *"Fine. You win."* — Loc
- In `tomorrow`, show it greyed out with "This is tomorrow's, if you go downstairs." At `first-morning`, show the real one with a share button.
- **Why:** Wayk spends five onboarding screens on receipt styles (rows 39–43) and shows a success-stats card after each mission (row 74). It gives the morning a reward that isn't a streak or a punishment, and it is free video and screenshot material for the marketing plan.
- **Brand check:** no sleep score, no sleep duration claims. It reports only *apps* asleep and the wake method. One style; don't add a style picker.

### 8. Show real usage after `apps` (needs a feasibility check)
- **What:** after `screen-time` and `apps`, render a DeviceActivityReport view filtered to the chosen apps for last night after their bedtime: **"Last night after 11:30: 52 minutes. TikTok 38, Instagram 14."** Loc: *"You guessed 30–60. Honest. I'm almost impressed."*
- **Why:** RISE's strongest pre-paywall moment is "8 DAYS FOUND", computed from your own data (`showcase-4.jpg`). Real numbers beat estimates, and ShutEye's timestamped events (`showcase-3.jpg`) show how concrete times sell.
- **Risk:** DeviceActivityReport renders inside a sandboxed extension. The app can display the view but can't read the numbers, which is consistent with our "I never see it" copy. Its data is sometimes empty or late on first authorization. Build a graceful fallback (skip the screen when empty) and verify on device before committing to it.

### 9. Echo the `time-back` answer inside `reveal`
- **What:** under "7½ hours a week on your phone in bed", add one Loc line built from `time-back`: e.g. "Slow mornings" → *"That's 16 days of slow mornings a year. I'd take them."* For "Read": *"About 20 books a year. Or one, slowly. Your call."*
- **Why:** Wayk turns the goal answer into a calculated payoff (row 22). Right now `time-back` is answered and then never mentioned again. Only `tried` gets an echo.

### 10. Open `offer` with a row that is already done
- **What:** keep the dated timeline (grid5) and add a first row with a check mark: **"Tonight's plan: set ✓"** (11:30 PM, 3 apps, downstairs). Then Today / Day 5 / Day 7 as now.
- **Why:** Sleep Cycle `showcase-5.jpg` starts its trial timeline with a ticked step, so the user enters the paywall already in motion. It is a small change with honest content, because it is true.

### 11. (Minor) The `commit` card fills in line by line
- **What:** when `commit` appears, the summary card's rows (lights out, alarm, method, apps) appear one after another in about 1 s, each with a quiet check. This is real data, so there are no fake percentages.
- **Why:** the "everything is ready" checklists in Loóna (`showcase-4.jpg`), Wayk (`showcase-6.jpg`) and RISE (`showcase-4.jpg`). Locturne's hold-to-agree already beats Wayk's signature pad (row 37), so keep it.

## 3. Don't copy

- **Proof walls when there's no proof:** "#1 Alarm App in 97 countries", "Only alarm listed in medical journals" (Alarmy `002–004.jpg`), laurels and "1 Billion+ nights" (ShutEye `005–007.jpg`). We have no such numbers, and inventing them is a rejection and trust risk.
- **Rating prompts in onboarding:** Wayk rows 33–34. Apple now rejects these.
- **Fake urgency and discounts:** ShutEye's countdown discount (row 33) and "Sleep Day Deal 50% OFF" (`showcase-6.jpg`). Struck-through anchor prices: Alarmy `showcase-4.jpg` ($107.88 crossed out).
- **Fake progress loaders padded with social proof:** ShutEye rows 22–23 (14% → 69%).
- **Permissions before value:** notifications on launch (ShutEye `002–003.jpg`), ATT on the second screen (Wayk `004.jpg`). Locturne's current approach of asking at `armed` with reasons is better.
- **Asking for the name:** Loóna `004.jpg`. It breaks our own promise on `deal` ("No name, no email"), and Loóna's revenue shows name-sprinkling doesn't convert by itself.
- **Personalization theatre:** favourite colours and narrative style (Loóna rows 14–15), gender and student status (RISE rows 9–11). These are questions whose answers never visibly change anything. Locturne's own `age` ("Sleep needs change with age") is close to this. Cut it, or make the answer show up later.
- **Sleep metrics:** sleep need, sleep debt, energy potential (RISE rows 30–42), quality scores (Sleep Cycle `004.jpg`, ShutEye row 51). Locturne does not track sleep. Steal the timeline *form*, not the metric.
- **Gamified pet, points, tarot, quotes:** Alarmy rows 89–121. Off-brand clutter, and a points economy is a timed-unlock mechanic in disguise.
- **Money penalties:** Alarmy row 130, "self-penalty $1 per cheat". A punishment mechanic.
- **Glows:** the rising sun halo (Wayk `002–003.jpg`), the orange glow on Start (Sleep Cycle `005.jpg`), the light beam (ShutEye `006.jpg`), purple glowing buttons (Loóna). All banned by the no-glow rule.
- **Account walls before value:** Sleep Cycle `008.jpg` and row 9, a password form. Locturne needs no account.
- **50-screen flows:** Wayk (about 50) and RISE (about 48) work for them, but the top earner, Sleep Cycle, reaches its paywall in 12. Length isn't the lever. Visible personalization is.
- **Referral-code fields in onboarding:** Wayk row 31.

## Single most important insight

The sleep and alarm apps that feel personal **turn the user's own clock times
into a picture**: Wayk's typical-vs-Wayk morning, Wayk's tomorrow plan, RISE's
"see the future" timeline. They also **ask for the time first**: it is Alarmy's
first question and Sleep Cycle's first screen. Locturne asks bedtime and wake at
steps 14–15, so its first 13 screens can't say "11:30" or "7:00" and read like a
generic quiz. Ask for the times first, then let every screen speak in them, and
replace `math` with a drawn version of *their* night.
