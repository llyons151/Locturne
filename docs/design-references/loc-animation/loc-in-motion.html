What the research said, and where it shows up

A skeleton, not a cut-out

The silhouette is a texture on a mesh of about 2,400 points, each weighted to body, head, ear, cheek-fluff and paw bones. Where two bones meet, the shape bends smoothly instead of hinging. This is Rive and Spine’s binding and weighting.

Successive breaking of joints

Every move travels down the chain: the body leans first, the head follows about 60 ms later, the ears about 120 ms later. Animators stagger keys like this by hand; here it’s built in.

Physics on the loose parts

The head, ears and fluff each have a spring with inertia, strength and damping (Spine’s physics constraints). They lag, overshoot and settle on their own, so a snap shakes the ears and a drop lifts them.

Planted, then bent

His paws are weighted to the ledge, so they stay put. Posture changes squash and stretch the body from the ledge instead of sliding him up and down.

Never quite still

A slow weight shift runs under everything (a moving hold), with the head countering the body. Breathing moves the chest first and the ears last.

Keyframed beats on top

The bits are still timed beat by beat with their own easing. The skeleton turns those values into a body that bends, and the physics adds the follow-through.
