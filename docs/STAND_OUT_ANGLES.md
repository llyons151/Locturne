# Angles to stand out

Written October 1, 2026. The question: which angles make Locturne successful,
useful and different from the other apps in the category? This builds on
[STRATEGY_DEEP_DIVE.md](STRATEGY_DEEP_DIVE.md) (Sept 30) and
[GAME_PLAN.md](../GAME_PLAN.md), which stays the source of truth. Nothing here is
applied to the plan yet. Rankings and estimates are **[OPINION]**; market facts are
cited at the end.

## 1. What changed since September 30

The "apps stay locked until you're up" mechanic is now crowded. Four more apps
turned up in one search, on top of Unbed, Flint, SleepShield, Sunbreak and BedLock:

| App | Mechanic | Price | Traction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anchor** | AlarmKit alarm that rings through Silent and force-quit; dismiss by scanning a barcode, QR or NFC tag; apps stay locked on a schedule. 3 emergency overrides a month. "Set in Stone" stops edits 2 hours before the alarm. | $9.99/mo, **$59.99/yr**, $129.99 lifetime, no trial | New |
| **Clockblock** | Stopping the alarm blocks your apps. AlarmKit + Screen Time, 11 themes, fully on-device. | Free, **$5.99 one-time** | No ratings (Apr 2026) |
| **SleepLock** | Bedtime app lock with streaks, morning check-ins, "hours won back", badges. | $4.99/mo, **$9.99/yr**, $29.99 lifetime, $1.99/wk | No ratings (May 2026, updated last week) |
| **Flow Alarm** | Alarm stops only when you scan a physical dock. | Hardware | Unknown |

What this means:
- **The mechanic alone won't make you stand out.** Anchor already has your
  "passes" (overrides) and your "edits wait until later" rule (Set in Stone), at
  your price.
- **Most competitors are alarm-first.** Their lock starts when you wake. Almost
  none own both ends of the night in one loop, and none of them has a
  personality. They all read like utilities.
- **The bottom of the market is collapsing.** $5.99 one-time and $9.99/yr apps
  exist. You can't win on price, so the app has to feel obviously worth more.
- **Hardware is taken.** Brick, Bloom, Unbed, Flow and Anchor's tag all sell
  "the key is somewhere else". Steps are your answer to that with nothing to buy.
  Say so ("no tag, no dock, your legs are the key").

## 2. The angles, ranked

### 1. Loc is the product, not the decoration (strongest)

No one else in this category has a character. Duolingo shows that a mascot with
attitude can be the brand, the notifications and the marketing at once.
- It's the part competitors can't copy in a weekend. A feature list can be copied;
  a voice people quote can't.
- It turns every screen into content: the shield line, the morning roast, the
  share card.
- **Useful, not only funny:** his lines should carry the rules ("Nope. Changes
  wait until tomorrow night. I'm not negotiating with 2 a.m. you.").
- **Risk:** a joke heard 30 times gets annoying. The line bank needs size and
  rotation (VOICE.md). Track repeats per user and never show the same line twice
  in a week.
- **Next step:** make his real audio the first test, as planned. A 3-second
  sound that becomes a TikTok sound is the cheapest distribution there is.

### 2. "Awake isn't the problem. Getting up is."

Position against the alarm apps instead of next to them (sharpened Oct 1 after
review).
- Most wake-up apps are alarms first. Erly and Alarmy only make sure you wake
  up. Unbed, Anchor, Flow and Clockblock add app blocking, but it hangs off their
  alarm: you have to use their alarm, and the key is a single scan or tap.
  Clockblock's block even ends on a timer, so you can wait it out in bed.
- The person Locturne is for usually isn't oversleeping. They're awake at 7:00
  and still in bed scrolling at 7:50. An alarm app doesn't help them, and neither
  does a lock that ends at a clock time.
- Locturne needs no alarm. It works however you wake up, and only proof of
  being up (200 steps) opens the apps. It also covers bedtime, which the alarm
  apps mostly don't.
- **Lines to test:** "Awake isn't the problem. Getting up is." / "Other apps
  wake you up. Locturne gets you up."
- **Watch:** the one-tap-on-a-tag apps have the same back-to-bed hole that
  DESIRE_VALIDATION flags for you. Fixing it (the two-part wake-up) would be a
  real difference, not just a different key.
- Keep AlarmKit for the first update (as STRATEGY_DEEP_DIVE says), but lead with
  "your apps go to sleep", not "a better alarm". On "alarm" you're one of ten.

### 3. Honest reliability as the brand

Every review set in this category complains about the same two things: it can be
bypassed, or it glitched (Opal's daily limit misfiring, Unbed's NFC rescans and
the reinstall workaround). Nobody advertises honesty about it.
- **A weekly "Loc's report":** "Your lock held 7 of 7 nights. Monday I lost Screen
  Time access for 40 minutes; that's on Apple, and I told you at the time." No app
  currently tells you when it failed.
- Never claim "unbeatable". Claim "you'll always know if it isn't working".
- This also feeds the D30 gate: users who trust the lock keep it on.

### 4. Strict but fair (the middle the research points to)

The research summary: the hardest blockers get rage-quit, and the easiest ones
don't change behavior. Forced blocking works; opt-in blocking showed no effect.
Locturne's design already sits in the gap: a schedule you can't loosen from bed,
plus a few humane passes.
- Make that the pitch: "Strict at 2 a.m. Fair on sick days."
- **Neurodivergent users:** a UBC study found rigid blockers clash with ADHD.
  Revenge bedtime procrastination is also more common with ADHD. The accessible
  alternative and the passes aren't edge cases; say in onboarding that they exist.

### 5. College students as the beachhead

You're a student, so this is the audience you can reach and film for free.
- Revenge bedtime procrastination is the documented student pattern: a long day,
  then "my time" at 1 a.m. 8 a.m. classes are the morning hook.
- **Content:** dorm-room videos, "my phone won't work until I'm out of bed for my
  8am", roommates reacting to Loc.
- one sec runs a schools program and reports students getting 2+ hours more
  sleep a week. A campus ambassador or club partnership is an analog you could
  run on your own campus.
- **Price tension:** students are price-sensitive and SleepLock is $9.99/yr. If
  students convert badly at $59.99, test a student offer code before cutting the
  main price.

### 6. Evidence you own

one sec's moat is a Max Planck study (57% less social media use). Locturne can
run a small version.
- The v1.1 opt-in "went to bed earlier" dataset is the start. A short study with
  a professor or a psych class at your university is cheaper than it sounds and
  gives you a claim nobody else in the morning-lock niche has.
- Keep within the claims in VALIDATION_RESEARCH section 12 until there's data.

### 7. Shareable artifacts

The morning share card is in v1. Add a monthly "Loc's report card" in the same
voice ("31 nights. 6,412 steps before TikTok. You tried to change bedtime at
1:14 a.m. four times. I said no four times."). It's a free ad each time it's
posted, the way Spotify Wrapped is.

## 3. Angles to avoid

- **Hardware tags or docks.** Taken by Brick, Bloom, Unbed, Flow and Anchor, and it
  adds shipping and inventory.
- **A price war.** You lose to $5.99 one-time.
- **Stats and wellbeing scores.** That's Opal's lane and the plan already rules it out.
- **"Unbreakable" claims.** iOS lets anyone revoke Screen Time access. The first
  TikTok showing the bypass would sink the claim.

## 4. Suggested changes (not applied)

1. Add Anchor, Clockblock, SleepLock and Flow to the competitor list in
   GAME_PLAN ("Other indies are circling").
2. Make the positioning line "Awake isn't the problem. Getting up is." and test
   it against the two current lead hooks.
3. Add the weekly honest reliability report and the monthly report card to v1 or
   v1.1.
4. Treat college students as the launch audience for content, and plan one campus
   test (ambassador or club) before January.
5. Keep Loc's line bank big enough to avoid repeats; add a per-user repeat guard.
