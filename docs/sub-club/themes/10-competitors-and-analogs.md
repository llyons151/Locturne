# 10 · Competitors and close analogs (Opal, Rise, Calm, sleep / screen-time / wellness)

Cross-episode synthesis of Sub Club batches 01–10. Citation format `B04 · 2024-01-10`. "(?)" = garbled-caption number. Tags: **[Hard]** / **[Freemium]** / **[Both]**.

**Gap:** no episode covers an alarm, wake-up, or "get out of bed" app. The closest analogs are Opal (app blocking, scheduled sessions), Rise (sleep), Calm/Headspace (sleep content), Welltory (health behaviour that happens outside the app), RoboKiller/Nomorobo (value delivered in the background), and habit apps (Ladder, Duolingo, HabitKit, Zumba).

## Consensus

1. **Screen-time blocking is a growing, investable category and Apple's built-in Screen Time isn't a barrier.** [Both]
   - BlueThrone: Sensor Tower stacking shows the category grows with each new entrant; new apps capture untapped users without taking Opal's share (own analysis + opinion; B08 · 2025-10-15). GP Bullhound names screen-time/digital-focus as a category-killer opportunity (humans are bad at blocking distractions; awareness rising; teens key) (opinion; B08 · 2025-11-12). Opal 2026: vibe-coded clones help educate the market (B09 · 2026-04-29). Duolingo's CPO: their number-one competitor is screen time generally (B08 · 2026-01-21).

2. **Opal's hard-paywall playbook worked to ~$10M ARR; the generalisable parts are placement, a default schedule, and the timeline paywall.** [Hard] See details below.

3. **Sleep apps win when onboarding itself delivers a personal number/plan.** [Both] Rise is cited three times as a model (Phil Carter B03 · 2023-12-13; Sylvain B06 · 2025-03-14; Leon Sasson B09 · 2026-03-05).

## Opal (screen time / app blocking) — two episodes, closest analog

**2024-01-10, "Lessons from 121 A/B tests" (B04)** — iOS, paid acquisition, knowledge workers 25–40.
- Paywall early in onboarding, "not too early", at peak motivation: download→trial ~7% → ~17% (A/B, iterated). Two screens earlier ≈ +10% trial starts.
- Discreet/hidden dismiss button won; X at 80% opacity +20% revenue (source unclear (?)).
- Onboarding straight into a scheduled blocking session (user picks apps; default Mon–Fri 9–5; starts tomorrow automatically; easy to edit) — "huge win" (A/B).
- Every test adding choice in onboarding failed ("fatigue budget").
- Blinkist timeline paywall (trial today, reminder day 6, charge day 7) never beaten by dozens of challengers.
- Day-1 re-engagement mostly failed; trial-cancel 50%-off push + modal "works quite well".
- Tiered referral rewards (5 friends free year, 20 lifetime, prize at 50): +80% users sending ≥1 referral (A/B).
- Friends' screen-time social feature: slight uplift. Phone-number sign-in.
- Model: hard-ish paywall, pricey annual ($69 → ~$99–100), 7-day trial, 100%+ day-8 ROAS, $0 → $5M ARR in a year on paid, team of 7. "Gym membership effect." Weekly Monday releases judged on day-8 ROAS.
- Flow: current screen time → questions → mini report → lifetime projection (~22 yrs (?) at 5h08m/day) → "we can save you 3–5 years" → trial offer. Age question branches students to referral/free paths. "Where did you hear about us" used for attribution.
- Ads: mission-led creator videos that don't show the product; licensed a user's organic video.
- He says 121 tests was too many — should have prioritised bigger swings. Believed the annual hard-paywall funnel caps at ~$10–30M ARR; starting to move toward freemium (easier dismiss raised activation, lowered ARPU "manageably"). Paid penetration ~17% (?) of MAU.

**2026-04-29, "Why Opal stopped chasing revenue" (B09)**
- $10M ARR with ~11 people on a hard paywall, then true freemium. Payers/MAU ~20% → ~9%; DAU "exploded" past 1M; organic growth up; revenue grew ("pays back tenfold" — CEO claim, unquantified). 10M+ downloads.
- Students ~2/3 of DAU → Opal for Schools (per-student, from an inbound high-school request). Teen↔parent word of mouth.
- Free/paid line test: "Would a non-payer recommend the app? If not, give away more." Free "blocks" tested 1…several, settled on 3 (A/B series).
- Biggest win of year: a focus-based streak with custom animated flame + gems at milestones. Most-loved interaction: tapping to crack a gemstone (not measurable). Opal as tactile opposite of a warm glass phone, "said to calm the mind".
- Early retention claimed on par with Duolingo/Spotify/Strava (no number).
- Next barrier: people agree they overuse phones but balk at "an app that blocks apps" → growth needs no setup, less commitment, coaching over restriction.
- Viral user film ("your phone is a nuclear-grade weapon aimed against you") licensed as-is, seen by tens of millions. User-creator "Olivia Unplugged" channel 0 → ~700k followers (screen time, bedtime routines). Unbranded "Scrolling Kills" billboard: no measurable downloads, helped a school deal.
- "If your user wins, you win."

**Third-party mentions:** Opal ~$120/yr pitched as productivity ROI (BlueThrone, B08 · 2025-10-15); GP Bullhound guest uses Opal to shut off weekend email (B08 · 2025-11-12).

## Rise Science (sleep)
- Phil Carter's favourite onboarding: sleep framed as "a drug", one metric (sleep debt), a few questions, optional wearable, then personal sleep debt with bedtime, wake, caffeine cutoff in ~2 min — onboarding delivers the coaching (B03 · 2023-12-13). Sylvain names it a strong pre-paywall story (B06).
- Leon Sasson (B09 · 2026-03-05): porting app onboarding to web failed twice; web works as a separate channel after "hundreds" of iterations. Web audience older (40–60+), more female, lower intent, often higher WTP. **App onboarding = time-to-value; web onboarding = sell the problem** (help users realise tiredness is a sleep problem). Web: monthly/quarterly plans, discounted first period beats free trial (A/B). Persona funnels ("tired, wants morning productivity" vs "menopause sleep") are the "holy grail". Easy cancel is table stakes on web.

## Calm / Headspace
- Calm paywall tuned to the 1am insomniac (soft blue, calming language); customised by entry point (celebrity sleep story) (Phil Carter, B03). Calm plays rain sounds from first open and in ads (Mehta, B08 · 2025-11-26). Calm's "are you here to sleep, focus…?" tells users what the app does (Hudson, B06). Calm locks most meditations but shows the whole catalog (Steve Young, B03). Calm's daily gift uses a finger-circling bowl, on brand (Sylvain, B06). Calm's sleep stories were a "secondary PMF" adding a nightly trigger meditation lacked (Google Play, B04 · 2024-05-20). Calm's LeBron campaign cited as reaching a non-ICP audience (B09 · 2026-03-06 PhotoRoom). Daryl Stone ex-Calm credited for pre-paywall story thinking (B06).
- Headspace: different content for "sleep" vs "stress/anxiety" (Daphne, B07). Pray.com copied Calm/Headspace formats (bedtime Bible stories) to set benchmarks (B05).

## Other sleep / wellness / behaviour-change analogs
- **Rest** (Podcast App team): sleep audio + LLM sleep coaching; first users by cross-promoting to existing night listeners; US sleep market ~$30B/yr (?) (B03 · 2023-10-04).
- **Unnamed sleep app** used as the counterexample of flat personalisation (bedtime → only reminder time) (Hudson, B06 · 2025-01-08).
- **Welltory** (health tracking): no daily job in wellness; sleep happens outside the app so Duolingo-style shame loops fail; retention came from novelty and "magic" (zero-effort value); shame from failed goals drives churn (B04 · 2024-05-29). Host: "you can't get a better night's sleep in five minutes in the grocery line."
- **Sweatcoin**: reframe "you walk anyway, earn something"; growing balance; capped rewards (B06 · 2025-03-05).
- **RoboKiller / Nomorobo** ("works while you sleep" value): surface invisible blocked value via reports and notifications (B06 · 2024-12-11); 14-day trial because value may not appear early (B03 · 2023-11-01).
- **Monkey Taps** (motivation app): best ad ever was a screenshot of a notification (Petit, B03 · 2023-08-07).
- **222** named a strong pre-paywall onboarding (B06).
- **Habit apps:** HabitKit heavy New Year seasonality and underpricing (B05); Ladder streak + widget (B09); Duolingo reminders at 23.5h (B06).
- **Life360** (peace-of-mind safety): people pay for peace of mind only if the product keeps it top of mind; free-user bill of rights (B07 · 2025-05-14; B10).
- **Skylight** (family calendar): habits set in ~30 days (B09).
- **Panic-attack app** (Anya, via Steve Young): soft closable paywall doubled revenue; hard paywall avoided on ethical grounds (B03).

## Contested

**A. Should an app-blocking app stay hard-paywalled?** Opal says freemium unlocked students, schools and 1M+ DAU — but only after $10M ARR, brand and word of mouth. SOSA 2026: hard converts 5x with equal retention; Phil Carter saw a −50% freemium failure (B09). For a new entrant the evidence favours hard first (theme 01).

**B. Restriction vs coaching.** Opal 2026 says restriction-framed "an app that blocks apps" limits the next 100M users; growth needs coaching and less setup (B09). Opal 2024's biggest win was an automatic restriction schedule (B04). Both can be true at different stages.
