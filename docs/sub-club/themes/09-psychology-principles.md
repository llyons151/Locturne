# 09 · Psychology principles that show up in the data

Cross-episode synthesis of Sub Club batches 01–10. Citation format `B02 · 2023-07-12`. "(?)" = garbled-caption number. Tags: **[Hard]** / **[Freemium]** / **[Both]**. This file groups findings by mechanism; the underlying results also appear in the topic themes.

## Consensus

1. **Commitment / investment / sunk cost — friction that builds investment raises conversion.** [Both]
   - Burner: letting users pick their own number (investment) beat removing the screen (A/B; B02 · 2022-07-13). Lose It, Zumba, Coconote, Sesame: longer, belief-building onboarding raised conversion (A/B; theme 05). Duolingo: making users *choose* their reminder day (added friction) raised trial conversion (A/B; B09 · 2026-03-02). Seufert: a deliberate hurdle before the product can filter for intent (secondhand anecdote, ~40% ROAS (?); B08 · 2025-09-03). Mathias (Subway Surfers): frictionless journey with a few deliberate friction points (B08).
   - **Money as commitment:** Opal's "gym membership effect" — users pay partly to commit to changing (B04 · 2024-01-10). Mehta: gyms never give free trials because paying creates the commitment to show up (B08 · 2025-11-26). Babbel: longest/priciest plans stick longer "because there's money on the line" (observed, directional; B05 · 2024-08-07).
   - **Limit:** BoldVoice — forcing or tricking people into a trial after they've invested in onboarding lifts trials but gets paid back in refunds (A/B; B10 · 2026-09-16). Host anecdote: 60-question sunk cost + $1 plan + "continue" upsells → ~$350–400 charged, half recovered (B10 · 2026-07-22).

2. **Loss aversion and endowment — let users own something, then show what they'd lose.** [Both]
   - Lose It: configure a premium feature in onboarding, then "these features you set up are Premium" at the downgrade choice; only works for day-1-resonant features (A/B; B02 · 2023-07-12). Sylvain generalises it (B06 · 2025-03-14).
   - Duolingo: trials start more when framed as "keep your progress / finish this lesson" after running out of hearts (B09 · 2026-03-02); "your benefits have ended" drawer after a free taste (B09); streak repair purchase (B08).
   - Sweatcoin: users value their own coins 10–50x more than others' (survey); a growing balance instead of a resetting count creates lock-in (B06 · 2025-03-05).

3. **Clarity and reassurance beat persuasion at the purchase moment.** [Both]
   - Trial-timeline paywalls (+23–25% at None to Run; unbeaten at Opal; Duolingo) (theme 02). Duolingo: "our goal is not to persuade… it's clarity and confidence" (B09). Microsoft: "Buy now" under a trial headline makes people fear an instant charge (B04). Natal: "the hack is build trust" (B10). Built With Science credits "lower perceived risk" for its annual shift (B10).

4. **Defaults and decoys steer choice strongly.** [Both]
   - Opal default schedule (A/B, "huge win"); Weather Up default plan (~33% vs ~8% switching away); Microsoft pricier default lifted both plans; Skylight opt-out bundle; Tinder decoy pricing of 7-day Passport = 7-day Plus; Shawn Gong three tiers turn "whether" into "which" (theme 02, 04).

5. **Emotion over logic — users decide fast and don't read.** [Both]
   - Duolingo: people react emotionally on mobile ("$0.00" wins) (B08). Shawn Gong: ~1 second, no feature comparison reading (B09). Daphne: emotional drivers ("I feel insecure") beat functional ("get fit") (B07). Monarch: "five whys" led from "see everything" to "a feeling of control" (B04). Coconote: toy framing gets views, problem/identity framing gets payers (B09).

6. **Goal-setting with a concrete number and a projected outcome.** [Both]
   - Zumba defaults weekly goal to 3 with "others succeeded with 3" copy (B07). Ladder 3/week (B06, B09). Noom's goal date that improves as you answer (B02). Opal's lifetime-screen-time projection → "save 3–5 years" (B04). Rise's sleep-debt number (B03). Hudson: time-to-result charts in interludes (B06).

7. **Shame is a churn driver outside Duolingo-shaped products.** [Both]
   - Welltory: shame + streak works only when the task is done inside the app in minutes; for sleep/health the tired user avoids an app showing failed goals (observed; B04 · 2024-05-29). Sweatcoin: guilt messaging misses ~70% of people (B06). Baxter: guilt from unused value is a cause of subscription fatigue — reset expectations (B01 · 2021-12-13). Welltory admits it hasn't solved showing bad news without shaming.

8. **Scarcity framed as a "superpower" monetises.** [Freemium mostly]
   - Tinder: 1 free Super Like/day, taught as scarce to both sides (B02 · 2022-08-10); swipe limits for ecosystem health turned out great for monetisation — "superpowers" (B08 · 2026-01-07). LinkedIn "Top Choice" capped at 3/month → 46% higher recruiter reply (observed; B08 · 2025-09-17). Opal: 3 free blocks, power users pay for more (A/B series; B09 · 2026-04-29).

9. **Social proof must answer the user's specific objection.** [Both]
   - Hudson: "people like you who had X" beats "Join 20M users" for small apps (B06). Marcus Burke: target-demo reviews (B04). Quizlet: hyperlocal proof (classmates) (B06). Savvy Navvy: credible third-party endorsement (instructors) (B10). Affiliate paywall showing the referring creator's face (B10 · 2026-09-22). Japan wants heavy social proof, US doesn't (theme 02).

## Contested

**A. Streaks.** Opal's streak was its biggest win; Ladder's weekly streak + widget; Duolingo's streak economy — vs Welltory's shame finding and Daphne's "quite flawed" (theme 07). Resolution: streaks counted on the user's own achievable cadence (weekly, focus-based), celebrate rather than punish.

**B. Manipulation tolerance.** Sweatcoin: "people agree to be slightly manipulated" when the goal is theirs (B06). Opal 2026: "if your user wins, you win… build AI on the user's side" (B09). vs Life360: stacked small dark patterns erode trust invisibly to A/B tests (B07); Lennox: black-hat quiz funnels bring regulator attention (B10); Sylvain: stacked aggressive tactics feel scammy and raise churn (B06).

**C. Autonomy vs being told.** TripAdvisor: labelled personalisation lost on high-stakes decisions — users must feel they chose (B08). Opal/Natal: users want one thing chosen for them (defaults, recommended program). Resolution: recommend quietly, let editing be easy.

**D. Relief vs sales pressure.** Steve Young's panic-attack app avoided a hard paywall because someone mid-panic needs relief, not a purchase screen (B03 · 2023-08-23). Welltory's "relief" retention driver (B04). Calm's paywall tuned to the 1am insomniac (B03).

## Fresh-start / timing
- New Year is the biggest acquisition moment for habit apps (1SE, HabitKit). Ladder CTAs name a start date ("we start Monday") and 6-week seasons give recurring "start now" moments (B03, B06). Duolingo's seasonal returns (back to school, New Year) (B06).
