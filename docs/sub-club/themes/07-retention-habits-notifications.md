# 07 · Retention, habits, streaks, notifications, social

Cross-episode synthesis of Sub Club batches 01–10. Citation format `B06 · 2025-03-06`. "(?)" = garbled-caption number. Tags: **[Hard]** / **[Freemium]** / **[Both]**. Churn benchmarks and win-back are in theme 08; activation in theme 06.

## Consensus

1. **You can't stop churn once usage stops — retention follows the core habit.** [Both]
   - Petit: "you're not going to convince people not to churn if they stop using the app" (B02 · 2022-03-02). Tinder's Phil Schwarz: brand doesn't save churn (B02). Layfield: measure retention by a core action on a cadence; fixing activation always improves churn (B07 · 2025-04-16). Google Play/SOSA churn surveys: cost + "not using enough" ≈ 70% of reasons; "found a better app" small (B06, B09 · 2026-03-06). Baxter: if cancellers are non-users it's a product problem, not price (B01).
   - Ladder's Ben Gammon: "retention is king" (B09 · 2026-03-07). Opal: "retention is the ultimate proof of value" (B09 · 2026-04-29).

2. **Notification timing should follow behaviour, not a user-picked time.** [Both]
   - Duolingo: scheduling the next reminder 23.5 hours after the user's first practice of the day consistently beat user-set times and other strategies (many experiments; B06 · 2025-03-06). "As soon as you set a time, the likelihood you're free at that time forever is zero."
   - Zumba: pushes timed to each user's usual workout day/hour (B07 · 2025-08-06).
   - Carvell: impact = reach × relevance × frequency; highly relevant messages tolerate high frequency; SoundCloud messaging tests moved retention +5pp (observed; B01 · 2021-07-28).
   - Duolingo: one reminder per missed day for 7 days, then stop; if not back by day 3–4, "tough sledding" (observed; B06).
   - Caution: a new push gives a novelty bump that can turn negative after ~6 weeks from over-notification — use 3–6-month control groups (Duolingo, B08 · 2026-01-21). Life360: banners/pushes/emails become noise; a cute animated dog on app open drove feature adoption (B10 · 2026-05-13).

3. **Message after a completed session, on a high — never interrupt the core flow.** [Both]
   - Lose It!: interrupting logging caused churn and no upgrades; messages after the day's log worked much better; in-app ≈ 10x the revenue of email for the same offer (A/B-ish; B02 · 2023-07-12). Duolingo: ads only after a lesson ends (B08). Zumba: celebration + badges + rating + next class after each session (B07).

4. **Simple streaks and visible progress tied to the core behaviour retain — shame-based loops don't.** [Both]
   - Ladder: check mark per workout, 3 = weekly streak, home-screen calendar widget lights completed days; ~1/3 install it; the widget is a billboard friends notice (observed; B09 · 2026-03-07).
   - Opal: a focus-based streak shown as a custom animated flame with gem rewards at milestones was the "biggest win of the year" for retention and growth (observed, no number; B09 · 2026-04-29).
   - Duolingo: first-ever revenue was $1–3 to repair a broken streak (observed; B08). Sweatcoin: a growing balance instead of a daily count that resets creates lock-in (B06 · 2025-03-05).
   - *But* Welltory: shame-plus-streak loops only work when the task is finished inside the app in minutes (Duolingo); for health behaviours that happen outside the app (sleep), showing failed goals makes tired users avoid the app; "smarter" Apple-style rings caused shame and didn't retain (observed; B04 · 2024-05-29). Sweatcoin: guilt messaging doesn't reach the ~70% without intrinsic motivation (B06). Daphne calls streaks "quite flawed" (B07).

5. **Stored state and identity create lock-in.** [Both]
   - Ladder journal (~70–75% log) → PRs and progress messages (B09). Coconote's growing note archive + identity ("student who records every class") (B09). SOSA 2026: AI apps churn ~30% faster (yr-1 retention 21% vs 31%) — hosts attribute it to no stored state (B09). Crowley's "Maslow's hierarchy of subscription": identity/passion, community, leaderboards, fresh content (B05 · 2024-10-02).

6. **Social and household units retain best; structured social beats open chat.** [Both]
   - Ladder: coach-led team chats doing the same workout → consistency; getting users into chat earlier predicts completion; engineered "cheers" for new triallers (observed; B03 · 2023-11-29, B06). Life360 circles of 3–4 convert and retain best (B10). Simply: multi-profile family accounts retain best "by far" (B10 · 2026-06-24). Built With Science buddy add-on retains better (B10). LinkedIn dual plan retains better (B08). Equilab fall-alert safety contact deepens stickiness (B02 · 2022-04-13).
   - Zumba: separate chat threads about classes failed — users didn't want to talk after class (B07). Opal: seeing friends' screen time gave only a slight uplift (B04).

7. **Retention is capped by how long the user has the problem.** [Both]
   - Layfield: fitness, dieting, dating, language, meditation are short-to-medium needs; "short life cycle + small audience" is a sure way to die (B07). MileIQ: work-driven use retains for years (B02). Crowley: tourists churn, locals stay; cohorts flatten (100 → 50 → 45 → 43 → 42) (B01, B05). Reading.com "works itself out of a job" (B07). Burner: temporary need → 20–30% of new subs are returners (B08).

## Contested

**A. Referral programs.** Opal's tiered rewards (5 friends = free year, 20 = lifetime) gave +80% users who send ≥1 referral (A/B; B04 · 2024-01-10); Sweatcoin's invite-to-unlock bootstrapped growth (B06). vs Lose It referral broke even, lower-LTV referees, killed (B02); Life360 referral/gifting failed "across the board" (B10); Greg promo-code referrals too much friction (B01).

**B. Novelty vs consistency.** Welltory's retention boost came from a novelty system (~45 message types) (B04). Duolingo tests copy tone for 6 years with an ML bandit (B06). Ladder's ethos is the opposite: "don't make me think" (B09).

**C. Gamification.** Sweatcoin: works only when layered on something genuinely good for the user — "people agree to be slightly manipulated"; capping rewarded video at 1–3/day *improved* retention (test; B06). Opal's gem cracking is the most-loved interaction but not directly measurable (B09).

## Other tactics (single-source)
- Extrinsic triggers that are useful in themselves: widgets, live activities, real-event notifications (Phil Carter, B09 · 2026-04-15). Greg made plant care a "water reminder app" — reminders substitute for a missing emotional trigger (B01).
- Weekly progress-report emails comparing weeks (Duolingo B06); weekly stats recap (Zumba B07); Year in Review (Duolingo).
- Location-based "smart reminders" at the resort (Slopes, B02).
- "Since you last logged in, here's what changed" (Captions, B05); messaging benefits alone lifted retention (LinkedIn, B08).
- Seasonality: New Year is the peak for habit apps (1SE B01; HabitKit ~$60k in Jan–Feb vs ~$51k all 2023, B05). Ladder's 6-week seasons create "start now" dates (B03).
- Support as retention: Captions 58-second first response; cancelled-trial users jump the support queue (B05).
