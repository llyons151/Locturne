# 04 · Pricing, price changes, and plan mix

Cross-episode synthesis of Sub Club batches 01–10. Citation format `B09 · 2026-02-18`. "(?)" = garbled-caption number. Tags: **[Hard]** / **[Freemium]** / **[Both]**. Paywall layout of plans (annual-first, per-month display, defaults) is in theme 02.

## Consensus

1. **Most apps are underpriced; raising the price for new users usually holds conversion and retention.** [Both]
   - Skylight: $39 → $79/yr for new customers, minimal loss in attach rate or retention, "most transformative change to ARR"; $99 was slightly better on ARPU but rejected over "disgust" risk in interviews (A/B + interviews; B09 · 2026-02-18).
   - Lose It!: $39.99 was the equilibrium for 12 years across tests from $5 to $120; in late 2024 ~$80 broke even on net revenue for the first time, repeated on iOS/Android and new/existing/reactivated cohorts; full rollout matched the test; retention stable (A/B; B09 · 2026-03-03).
   - Coconote: $99.99 → $129/yr gave *more* users *and* more revenue (A/B; B09 · 2026-03-18, B10 · 2026-05-20).
   - MySwimPro: free → $10/mo → ~$100 → $120 → $180/yr; conversion "didn't really change much" (sequential + A/B; B04 · 2024-02-21). Apollo: raise to ~$30–40 (?) "basically no change" (anecdote; B05). Mojo: ~+50% ARPU (?) from price rises, Turkey +100% (B05 · 2024-07-10). Shot Pattern doubled to ~$75/yr after shipping features (B07).
   - Hosts' mottos: "keep doubling it until your haters folder gets too big"; "if somebody isn't mad about your pricing, it's wrong" (Jacob; B05). Ravi Mehta: anchor high ($20–30/mo if category allows) and optimise down; if nobody pays, it's a PMF problem (B08 · 2025-11-26). Founders undercharge from impostor syndrome; pricing is reversible (SOSA 2026, B09).

2. **Price elasticity is often flat within a reasonable range — revenue comes out about even.** [Both]
   - AllTrails: $30 vs $15/yr "about a wash" on revenue; picked $30 to leave room for discounts (A/B; B01 · 2021-11-17). PhotoRoom: doubling price roughly halves payers within ~10% (observed; B01 · 2021-09-27). Burner: $49.99/$59.99/$69.99 annual → LTV roughly break-even (A/B; B02 · 2022-07-13). Lose It's 12-year equilibrium (above). Baxter: many products are inelastic; low conversion is usually not a price problem (B01 · 2021-12-13).

3. **Grandfather existing users; raise for new users.** [Both]
   - Skylight (honoured old price indefinitely, gave up "a couple million"), Lose It (Apple's opt-in increase flow is confusing and untestable), Slopes, Astropad, Genius Scan, Rapchat all grandfathered and saw little backlash (B09, B02, B01, B06). Counter-data: Talking Parents doubled price for *existing* subs ($4.99 → $9.99), expected to lose half, lost about a quarter (observed; B03 · 2023-08-09 — but ~60% of users are court-ordered). Reid DeRamus: most revenue from a raise comes from existing subs; grandfather only very early (B03 · 2023-09-06).

4. **Anchor the price to a real-world analog the user already pays for.** [Both]
   - Slopes "season pass" (B02 · 2022-03-30); Fishbrain "a fish finder on your phone" vs ~$300 hardware (B01 · 2022-01-19); Opal ~$120/yr framed as productivity ROI for knowledge workers (B08 · 2025-10-15) and as a "gym membership effect" (B04); Built With Science "a personal trainer in your pocket" at $189/yr (B10); BoldVoice vs 1:1 coaching (B10); Tinder heavy users vs ~$400/mo on dates (B08); Spotify/Netflix ~$10/mo as the consumer ceiling (PhotoRoom B01, Ladder B03).

5. **Annual plans are prized for cash and payback; monthly for learning.** [Hard especially]
   - AllTrails: "annual is magic" (B01). Skylight: get payback on day zero with ~$60–70/yr upfront (B09). BoldVoice: annual-first reveals LTV/CAC per channel immediately (B10 · 2026-09-16). Mojo: yearly simplified 30-day UA payback (B05). Savvy Navvy: 2-year plan at ~30% off (web) — same LTV but cash upfront transformed CAC payback (observed; B10 · 2026-08-05). Opal: pricey annual, 100%+ day-8 ROAS (B04).

6. **Price tests need scale and must be judged on renewals/ARPU, not conversion.** [Both] See theme 12. Petit: ~400 (?) renewal pairs needed (B02 · 2022-03-02); Seufert: test durations before price points at small scale (B02); Flo: judge by ARPU — half price gave 3x retention and higher ARPU (B06 · 2025-03-04); Mojo rejected a day-7 winner on 7-day cancel rate (B09); Yousician: raises look like wins until control beats them at ~6-month renewal (B09).

7. **Surveys (Van Westendorp) are a useful starting point but stated WTP ≠ behaviour.** [Both]
   - Ladder: Van Westendorp (~500 responses) + ~100 calls → $60 → $29.99/mo, biggest month (B03 · 2023-11-29). UDocs: VW + conjoint → single tier, lower annual: +12% conversion, +10% net revenue (B03 · 2023-12-13). MySwimPro: ~20% of payers say $200–300 (B04). Caution: Reid DeRamus (B03), Tinder Select failed despite survey interest (B09 · 2026-03-04), Duolingo tutors (B08).

## Contested

**A. Lower price retains better vs premium price signals value.**
- *Lower:* SOSA 2025 p.71 — lower-priced apps retain better after yr 1; Barnard's $40/yr weather app ~40% retention, suspects lower would help (correlational; B06 · 2025-03-17). Flo half price → 3x retention (B06). Reading.com chose $12.49 over $14.99–19.99 because higher prices spiked LTV then faded over 2 years (A/B; B07 · 2025-05-28). Zumba cut $19.99/$129 → $14.99/$99 (B07). Cal AI ~$30/yr cited (B08, B09). Weather Up $40 too high in a commodity category (B04). V1: don't maximise the first price ("commission breath") (B05).
- *Premium:* Coconote — premium signals reliability (B09); Lose It moved to ~$80 partly because $40 signalled low value (B09); Mehta anchor high (B08); Babbel — users on longest/priciest plans stick longer "because money is on the line" (B05).
- Context: commodity or kids/parent categories favour moderate; painkiller/identity/professional categories tolerate premium (Daphne: once an app is a "painkiller", price sensitivity drops, B07 · 2025-05-02).

**B. Annual vs monthly LTV.**
- Annual wins: Weather Up monthly default cost ~60% early LTV (B04); None to Run yearly LTV beats monthly despite half monthly-rate (B02).
- Monthly wins: Simply — monthly beats annual on LTV in mature apps with strong retention (B10 · 2026-06-24). PhotoRoom led with monthly to learn from churners (B01). Barnard pays Ladder monthly because a $130 lump "feels big" (B08 · 2025-11-12). Ladder shows only monthly to zero-workout triallers (B06).
- Annual has a hidden cost: annual auto-renew turn-offs peak in month 1 (34% of eventual annual churners; SOSA 2026, B09) — see theme 08.

**C. Tiers.** See theme 02 Contested A. Onboarding-based: Daphne "don't add tiers too early" (B07); onX tiers by a dimension users understand (geography) (B06 · 2025-03-07).

**D. Lifetime plans.**
- Barnard: lifetime buyers are the highest-intent, so average-LTV pricing undercharges them; useful as anchor/off-ramp. Ronge: at ~50% annual churn LTV ≈ 2x annual price. Jacob: LTV "finds a level" (B01 · 2022-01-05). BlueThrone: juicing lifetime before a sale kills future monetisation (B08). Yousician: lifetime tests always "lose" upfront unless compared against projected LTV (B09).

**E. Regional pricing.** Flo: brute-force price cells per region; Brazil price cut → hundreds of % growth, Android optimal only ~15–20% below iOS in rich countries (B06). Mojo: lower LatAm prices beat Apple defaults (B09). Simply: PPP index → "dramatic" ARPU rise (B10). Layfield: almost always raises revenue but you maintain ~10 packages forever (B07). RevenueCat hosts: may just discount middle-class buyers who'd convert anyway (B04). Relevance for a US-first launch: low.

## Upsells and add-ons (single-source, observed)
- Post-purchase one-time upsell: Condé Nast ~5% take a higher tier (+$100–200 LTV) (B08 · 2025-10-29); Reading.com $9.99 printables ~20% (B07).
- Two-person plans: LinkedIn dual plan 20% off retains better (B08 · 2025-09-17); Built With Science gym buddy ~15% of trial starters, both 15% off (B10).
- Price-increase comms: explain it, restate value, small steps, time to a launch, never blame inflation (Reid DeRamus B03; Crowley B05; V1 "only the first point is heard" B05).
